CANADA — BRITISH COLUMBIA / NOVEMBER 9 DECISION

Millar Western idles Quesnel River Pulp indefinitely

Millar Western has decided to idle its Quesnel River Pulp mill in British Columbia indefinitely, extending a temporary shutdown that began September 4. In a November 9 letter to employees obtained by the Quesnel Cariboo Observer, the company cited market conditions and uncertainty about securing fiber at a workable cost.

The company plans to wind down remaining operations over several weeks and leave the mill in a condition that could allow a restart. It gave no restart date and said affected employees would lose their jobs and receive severance and other support.

Millar Western lists the Quesnel site at 375,000 air-dried metric tonnes of softwood pulp a year. That is the mill’s stated annual scale; the November decision does not quantify the additional output or fiber purchases lost beyond the temporary stoppage.

U.S. — PACIFIC NORTHWEST / NOVEMBER 9 ANNOUNCEMENT

ForestryWorks adds a partner for three Northwest states

The Western Forestry & Conservation Association has joined the ForestryWorks network for Washington, Oregon and Idaho. In their November 9 announcement, the association and the Forest Workforce Training Institute said the partnership will connect employers, educators and job seekers with the network’s job board and career materials.

The announcement covers careers in logging, forest management, conservation and manufacturing. It describes access to recruiting and career resources, with no new class dates or training seats announced for the three states.

Under the Radar

U.S. — CALIFORNIA / NOVEMBER 9 RELEASE

CAL FIRE releases the first chapters of its forest assessment

CAL FIRE announced the first public release of its 2025 Forest and Rangeland Assessment on November 9. The initial chapters cover forest resilience, rangelands, wildfire and risks to communities. The assessment tracks more than 50 indicators, including fire severity, tree mortality and water resources.

The agency is publishing the findings in batches and expects the compiled report in the second quarter of 2027. The chapters already online provide statewide context for forest and fuels planning; they do not authorize work on any individual tract.

Action Detector

U.S. — TRIBAL FORESTRY / JANUARY 26 DEADLINE

Tribal forest restoration grants offer a December draft review

The U.S. Forest Service’s Landscape Scale Restoration competition for Tribes lists grants of US$100,000 to US$300,000, with a match required. Eligible applicants include federally recognized Tribes and qualifying Tribal organizations that apply at a Tribe’s request with a letter of support.

The agency is seeking collaborative projects that put a restoration strategy into practice, such as reducing wildfire risk or improving watershed function. Applicants can submit a draft narrative and budget to their regional contact by December 11 for optional review. Final applications are due by email January 26, 2027, at 11:59 p.m. Eastern, with a copy to the appropriate Forest Service contact.
