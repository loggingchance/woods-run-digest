OREGON — TIMBERLAND / NOVEMBER 25 ACQUISITION

New Forests buys Oregon timberland with a carbon project planned

New Forests has completed the purchase of 9,300 acres in Josephine County, Oregon. Its November 25 announcement says the property includes Douglas-fir forest and habitat for northern spotted owls and Pacific martens.

The investment manager plans to develop a carbon project under California’s Cap-and-Invest Program and explore biodiversity and conservation opportunities alongside investment returns. The announcement does not disclose a seller, price, harvest schedule or new timber-supply commitments. The carbon project is a stated plan, not an announcement of issued credits.

The purchase adds to a U.S. portfolio spanning Washington, Oregon, Northern California and Oklahoma. It follows a separate 44,000-acre Olympic Peninsula acquisition in June.

Trade Policy Watch

  • The U.S. Trade Representative proposed additional Section 301 tariffs November 25 following manufacturing-capacity investigations involving 14 economies, including China, Vietnam and the European Union. Canada is not among those 14. These are proposals for public comment, with economy-specific exclusions and treatment of existing duties; they do not establish a new final tariff on Canadian lumber. Wood-product importers need the applicable product annexes and eventual final action before treating a proposed headline rate as a landed-cost change. Mills & Wood Markets

QUEBEC — PROPOSED MILL RESTART / NOVEMBER 26 REPORT

Témiscaming proposal connects a mill restart with propellant production

Temiscaming Precision Industries has proposed restarting the Témiscaming mill’s paperboard and high-purity cellulose lines, Peter Saunders reported November 26 in Pulp & Paper Canada. The company wants to convert cellulose into nitrocellulose propellant at a new plant south of the mill.

The proposal seeks provincial and federal support, Canadian control and Indigenous participation. It adds a specific product-development plan to earlier local efforts to buy and reopen the facility. The report does not establish a completed acquisition, financing, government customer contract or restart date. For wood suppliers, this remains a proposed return of an industrial outlet rather than confirmed buying.

CANADA — EXPORT TRACEABILITY / DECEMBER 8 ONLINE

Workshop follows wood from harvest plots to export documents

BC Wood is accepting registrations for a December 8 online workshop on forest geolocation and supply-chain traceability, from 11:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. Pacific. The listed cost is C$22 per person.

The session introduces QWEB’s Forest Geolocation platform, harvest-plot mapping, chain-of-custody records and GeoJSON files for European buyers. It is aimed at exporters and businesses supplying them. The organizer’s page provides registration and payment options. Workshop participation does not itself certify compliance; applicable product coverage and legal dates require the current regulatory guidance.

Under the Radar

COLORADO — FOREST HEALTH / NOVEMBER 24 REPORT RELEASE

Colorado’s forest-health roadmap includes wood markets

Colorado’s Department of Natural Resources released its 2055 Vision for Forest Health report November 24. The state’s announcement, reproduced by Coyote Gulch, identifies 22 goals and 71 actions, including consistent funding and forest-product markets alongside resilient watersheds and fire-adapted communities.

The roadmap calls for work across public, private and Tribal ownerships and is intended to complement local priorities. This is release of a long-range framework developed by the Forest Health Council, not an award of treatment contracts, a new funding intake or a harvest authorization.
