ROCKIES — WORKING FORESTS

New funding adds forest restoration crews in Idaho and Wyoming

The Nature Conservancy and the U.S. Forest Service announced a US$2.1 million commitment to expand restoration near communities in the Caribou-Targhee, Payette and Salmon-Challis national forests in Idaho and the Bridger-Teton in Wyoming.

The Nature Conservancy says its crews will help thin dense vegetation, prepare and carry out prescribed burns, and monitor their effects. The agreements add people to do the work. The announcement does not advertise timber sales or list contracts for bidding.

CANADA — BIOMASS / APPLICATIONS DUE DECEMBER 3

Canadian forest regions can apply for biomass site assessments

The Canada Forest Innovation and Renewable Markets Challenge will select 15 Canadian regions for site and biomass supply assessments and introductions to potential developers. Applicants need a named industrial site and at least 50,000 tonnes of surplus forest biomass a year within a 120 km drive. The application also calls for five support letters, including two from biomass suppliers.

Eligible applicants include Indigenous governments, local governments, producer groups and forest-sector facility operators. Ecostrat lists an information webinar November 17 at 1 p.m. Eastern. Applications close December 3 at 5 p.m. Eastern.

Under the Radar

MID-ATLANTIC — URBAN FORESTRY

Delaware pairs school tree grants with planting help

Delaware’s Forest Service reported November 10 that it helped plant eight trees at Lombardy Elementary School in Wilmington on November 5, following 12 planted last fall. The school used urban and community forestry grant funding, and agency staff helped with planting technique.

The agency points communities and organizations to its grant program for work that can include planting, maintenance, hazardous tree removal and urban forest management. The school announcement describes an existing award; it does not set a new application deadline.
