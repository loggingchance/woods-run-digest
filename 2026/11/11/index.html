THE FOREST BUSINESS SCHOOL

Free Rural Entrepreneurship Short Course starts November 30

A business idea needs time and attention before it can become a useful new service or a workable enterprise. The Forest Business School’s free Rural Entrepreneurship Short Course offers four live, remote discussions to help participants develop practical ideas for a business, a new service or an initiative within their organization.

Offered in partnership with The Northeastern Loggers’ Association, the course meets on Mondays, November 30 and December 7, 14 and 21, starting at 7 p.m. Eastern. Participants will connect ideas from Company of One by Paul Jarvis and The Minimalist Entrepreneur by Sahil Lavingia to their forest business experience. Individual consultations with Dr. Steven Bick, CF, will be arranged separately.

The course is open only to U.S. residents living in rural communities with a population of 50,000 or less. Participants should be able to attend all four sessions, prepare the reading and join the discussions.

Mill Operations

U.S. / CANADA — NOVEMBER 9 COURT FILINGS

Mercer files its prepackaged Chapter 11 plan

On November 9, Mercer International and one non-operating subsidiary filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions in the Southern District of Texas and submitted a prepackaged reorganization plan. The filing moves the recapitalization announced November 2 into court; the plan still needs court approval.

Mercer says its operating subsidiaries are not parties to the cases and will continue ordinary operations. The company says it intends to fund operations during the cases with cash on hand and cash generated by the business.

CANADA — NEWFOUNDLAND AND LABRADOR / NOVEMBER 9 REPORT

Sexton pauses log buying and harvesting after mill fire

Sexton Lumber has suspended sawmill operations, log purchasing and timber harvesting while it assesses damage from an October 29 fire at its Bloomfield facility. CBC reported November 9, citing the Newfoundland and Labrador Forestry Industry Association, that the fire caused substantial smoke damage and that no restart date had been set.

The association put the sawmill’s annual capacity at about 65 million board feet. That is capacity, not an estimate of production lost. The pause affects the mill’s suppliers and contractors as well as its lumber and agricultural customers.

Under the Radar

U.S. — NORTHEASTERN CALIFORNIA / NOVEMBER 10 DECISION

BLM approves ponderosa pine thinning near Adin

The Bureau of Land Management approved thinning and fuels work on about 304 acres of public land southwest of Adin, California. The Letterbox project aims to restore a more open ponderosa pine forest and reduce the vegetation that can carry fire into the crowns.

BLM expects work under a stewardship contract to start in fall 2026 or spring 2027 and finish by 2029. The November 10 announcement is a project decision and schedule estimate; it does not invite bids.

Action Detector

U.S. — GEORGIA / BIDS DUE DECEMBER 9

Allen Mountain offers a pine and hardwood timber sale

The Georgia Forestry Commission is accepting bids for the Allen Mountain timber sale, No. 11656, on 141.3 acres of U.S. Forest Service land southwest of Toccoa in Stephens County. The November 10 packet designates 127 acres for operator-selected commercial thinning.

Bids close December 9 at 11 a.m. Eastern. The packet gives separate instructions for mailed bids and the public opening at the Habersham County unit in Clarkesville; it also provides tract maps and operating requirements.
