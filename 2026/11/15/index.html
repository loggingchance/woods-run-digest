CANADA — FOREST CONSERVATION

Canada pledges up to C$560 million for tropical forests

Prime Minister Mark Carney announced on November 15 that Canada would contribute up to C$560 million over four years to the Brazil-led Tropical Forests Forever Facility. He made the announcement after meeting Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva in Manila.

Ottawa says the initial commitment will help the facility raise enough capital to begin operating and draw further investment. The facility is designed to use net investment returns to pay eligible tropical forest countries based on conservation performance. Canada counts the pledge toward its international climate finance goal for 2031.

Sunday Feature

The couple giving Atlanta’s fallen trees a second life

Kelly Syed came to woodworking from ceramics, painting and jewelry, taking a bowl-turning class at Decatur Makers. She and her husband Ali were making furniture and ornaments when wood prices climbed. Georgia Public Broadcasting reported in August that, at a friend’s suggestion, they turned to donated fallen trees. Their nonprofit, Atlanta Wood Foundation, runs a sawmill and makes salvaged lumber available to local artists and woodworkers. Kelly told GPB that finding trees was easier than getting people through the door. The foundation also offers classes, giving more people a way to work with the wood it saves.

MID-ATLANTIC — MASS TIMBER MARKETS

Washington, D.C. helps developers test mass timber before they build

The Washington Architectural Foundation’s new accelerator has US$160,000 to support early design and feasibility work on active projects in the District. Teams can study costs, construction schedules and building performance, with technical help from WoodWorks.

The awards support analysis and design; they do not represent construction contracts or committed wood orders. The foundation’s call provides project eligibility and submission instructions.

ROCKIES — BIDS OPEN NOVEMBER 19

Montana’s Mount Johns timber sale includes 12 miles of road maintenance

Montana’s Department of Natural Resources and Conservation opens bids at 2 p.m. Mountain on November 19 in Missoula for the Mount Johns sale near Lakeside. The prospectus lists seven ground-based units, about 12 miles of road maintenance and 0.6 miles of temporary road construction.

Bidders must supply a US$23,350 deposit or an accepted bid bond. The purchaser bids by the ton and must make a separate advance stumpage payment before cutting, based on six weeks of expected harvesting.

WESTERN CANADA — URBAN FORESTRY

Vancouver campus tree count becomes a long-term field record

The University of British Columbia reported November 13 that students recorded 6,737 trees and fully measured 6,098 on the core academic grounds of its Vancouver campus. Seven student cohorts built the inventory, using mapped locations and unique identifiers for individual trees.

With the baseline complete, the team plans to remeasure trees to track growth and health. The field record can also help check remote measurements made with lidar.
