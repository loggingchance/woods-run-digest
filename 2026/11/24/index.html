NEW BRUNSWICK / MAINE — NOVEMBER 20 BUYER BULLETIN

Before You Cut, Know Who Will Take the Wood

Two changes in the SNB Forest Products Marketing Board’s November 20 bulletin show why an outlet needs checking before a harvest starts. Woodland Pulp in Maine resumed hardwood pulpwood buying after a softwood run. Separately, the board’s defined contract with Louisiana-Pacific in Houlton, for eight-foot poplar through December 31, lost its fuel surcharge effective November 23. Producers who committed volume are asked to coordinate deliveries and trucking with the board.

Those are specific purchasing and contract conditions, rather than general bids available to every supplier. Before cutting, confirm accepted species, dimensions and grades, contamination limits, allocation and delivery slots, haul costs and any fuel surcharge. Identify a fallback outlet for material the primary buyer cannot take.

Keep the products separate: roundwood pulpwood, chips and mill residuals, and manufactured lumber have different customers. Pallet stock and ties must meet their own specifications; urban wood acceptance and mass-timber lumber grades do not make either a generic destination for pulpwood.

Working Forests & Wood Supply

GREAT LAKES — LOGGING CAPACITY / NOVEMBER 23 REPORT

Michigan’s wood supply depends on the crews that can deliver it

Michigan State University forest economist Shivan Gc told Capital News Service that losing local logging capacity could make mills seek wood farther away, increasing transportation costs even when manufacturing continues. The November 23 report connects a study of logging’s economic contribution with interviews about equipment costs, weak markets and longer hauls.

The researchers’ loss-of-capacity models are scenarios, not predictions of company closures. The distinction matters to buyers: keeping a mill running and retaining a dependable local harvesting supply chain are different measures of industry health.

Action Detector

U.S. — RESTORATION-WOOD PROCESSING / EXISTING LOAN PROGRAM

TPEP remains outside USDA’s separate B&I submission pause

USDA’s November 20 lender FAQ explicitly excludes the Timber Production Expansion Guaranteed Loan Program (TPEP) from the separate Business & Industry submission pause. Approved lenders can use TPEP for qualifying projects that establish, reopen, expand or improve a sawmill or other wood-processing facility using National Forest System restoration byproducts. Its FAQ specifies a 90% guarantee to the lender; this is a loan program, not a grant or a promise of approval.

Start with USDA’s priority-area map and an approved lender. The governing notice defines proximity as within 250 miles of a high- or very-high-priority restoration area, but also requires qualifying restoration byproducts, significant federal or Tribal raw material and a substantial reduction in restoration costs. Location alone does not establish eligibility.

Under the Radar

CANADA — ONTARIO / OPEN RAILWAY-TIE PROCUREMENT

Ontario Northland’s tie purchase comes with four addenda

Ontario Northland’s RFQ 2026 082 for supply and delivery of railway ties closes November 30 at 2 p.m., according to its current listing. Four addenda accompany the October 29 solicitation, including one dated November 23.

The agency requires all documents to be downloaded. Interested suppliers should obtain the packet and amendments through its access form and confirm the applicable tie specifications and submission instructions. This is a purchase of specified railway ties, rather than an outlet advertised for unsorted logs or pulpwood.
