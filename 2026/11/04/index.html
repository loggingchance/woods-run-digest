MIDWEST — MICHIGAN / NOVEMBER 3 RESEARCH REPORT

Michigan tests biochar markets for storm-damaged timber

Michigan State University researcher Emily Silver will examine whether storm-damaged timber and other low-value wood can find buyers as biochar. A US$300,000 U.S. Department of Agriculture grant supports the work, described in a November 3 report by MSU Extension’s Jack Falinski.

The project addresses wood left by the March 2025 ice storms in northern Michigan. Silver says conventional forest-products markets absorbed substantial salvage material but became saturated, leaving a need for additional outlets.

The research will examine both getting wood processed into biochar and finding end users. Planned work includes demonstrations, logger training, economic-impact assessments and testing.

This is market-development research, rather than a newly operating plant or a guaranteed purchase program. Silver identifies developing demand and barriers to production and use as questions the project must address.

Under the Radar

Hawaii’s Arbor Day brings native trees and hands-on care to five islands

Hawaii will celebrate Arbor Day on November 6, with tree giveaways, community plantings and tree-care events throughout the month. The state’s Kaulunani Urban and Community Forestry Program announced activities on Hawaii Island, Maui, Molokai, Oahu and Kauai.

A November 6 native-tree giveaway in Kailua-Kona will include restoration professionals offering care advice. On November 7, Maui Nui Botanical Gardens will host tree-care demonstrations and native and canoe plant giveaways, while events on Oahu and Kauai offer plant adoption and educational activities. The state’s announcement links to the full event calendar.

Could winter firewood demand rival a pulp mill?

Eric Kingsley argues that expensive heating oil could push households in Maine, New Hampshire and Vermont to burn more firewood. In a November 2 analysis, he estimates that one additional cord per wood-using household would represent roughly 1.5 million green tons of hardwood demand, comparable to a pulp mill’s annual consumption.

This is a scenario built on household-use and cord-weight assumptions, not measured additional demand. Kingsley notes that newly harvested wood would not be seasoned for this winter: households would draw down existing supplies, then replenish them next spring or summer. He invites discussion of what that could mean for loggers and wood markets.

U.S. — WOOD PRODUCTS / NOVEMBER 2 RESULTS

Boise Cascade’s plywood prices rise as shipments fall

Boise Cascade’s third-quarter results show why revenue growth alone does not describe mill activity. Plywood selling prices rose 31% from a year earlier, while plywood sales volumes fell 7%.

Its Wood Products division reported US$461.2 million in sales, up 16%, and US$16.1 million in segment income, reversing a US$12.1 million loss. Higher plywood prices and engineered-wood volumes outweighed lower plywood shipments and higher per-unit conversion costs.

Laminated veneer lumber volumes rose 18% and I-joist volumes rose 10%; selling prices for both were unchanged year over year. The November 2 release reports these as third-quarter operating results, rather than a forecast of an industry-wide recovery.

CANADA — ONTARIO / IN PERSON AND ONLINE

Forest pest forum registration closes November 13

Registration for the 69th National Forest Pest Management Forum remains open through November 13. Natural Resources Canada organizes the November 17–19 meeting, with attendance in Ottawa or virtually through the Canadian Institute of Forestry.

Sessions run 8:30 a.m.–5 p.m. Eastern on November 17–18 and 8:30 a.m.–noon on November 19. The program covers pest conditions, control operations, monitoring technology and research findings.

All attendees must register, including Canadian Forest Service and Canadian Food Inspection Agency employees.
