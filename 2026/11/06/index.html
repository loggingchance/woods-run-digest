U.S. — ARIZONA / NOVEMBER 5 OPERATIONS ADVISORY

Arizona restoration work pairs timber supply with fuel reduction

Arizona’s Brentwood restoration project is scheduled to begin this month, with a 1,300-acre timber sale expected to supply nearly 40,000 tons of timber to the state’s forest industry. Nearly 400 additional acres of hand thinning are intended to protect the Black River watershed and prepare for prescribed fire. Work is scheduled through 2029.

Brentwood is on the Alpine Ranger District of the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest. It is the first state-federal Good Neighbor Authority project on that forest, allowing Arizona to undertake restoration on federal land.

A November 5 Arizona Game and Fish Department advisory highlights Brentwood alongside Jotter 1 on the Coconino National Forest and Log Springs 1 on the Prescott. Together, the projects cover nearly 7,000 acres. The state’s original October 6 plan scheduled Jotter and Log Springs to begin in mid-October; the new advisory says that work has begun.

The agencies say no forest closures accompany the projects, but machinery, noise and crews will affect access and recreation. The timber figure is a projected supply, not a report of completed harvest or new contract availability.

Canadian Sawmills

CANADA — NOVEMBER 4 STATISTICS RELEASE

Canadian sawmill output rises in August while shipments fall

Canadian sawmills produced 3.674 million cubic metres of lumber in August, up 4.0% from July but down 3.8% from a year earlier, Statistics Canada reported November 4.

Shipments moved in the opposite monthly direction: mills shipped 3.5992 million cubic metres, down 3.7% from July. Shipments were nevertheless 1.5% above August 2025.

These are quantities of lumber produced and shipped, not prices or mill revenue. The figures are not seasonally adjusted and remain subject to revision; the release also revises earlier observations. August’s monthly production gain should therefore be read alongside both the shipment decline and the year-over-year production decrease.

U.S. — NEBRASKA / NOVEMBER 5 RESEARCH REPORT

Flying-squirrel study puts mature trees and canopy under scrutiny

University of Nebraska–Lincoln researchers are examining which trees southern flying squirrels use on East Campus. Forester Lord Ameyaw and graduate researcher Cameron Lyons will assess tree age, species, canopy cover and cavities, the university reported November 5.

The team plans to add five nesting boxes to 14 already installed, using the habitat work to guide placement. Trail cameras also record activity by the nocturnal squirrels. A Mutual of Omaha’s Wild Kingdom grant supports the project through roughly the end of the academic year.

The work combines tree and wildlife research at the edge of the species’ range. It is a study of habitat preferences, rather than completed evidence for a particular forestry prescription. The campus population has not been definitively counted.

U.S. — PENNSYLVANIA / BIDS DUE NOVEMBER 20

Allegheny stewardship timber bids go through a partner solicitation

Bids for the Lamentation Run timber sale on Pennsylvania’s Allegheny National Forest are due November 20 at 10 a.m. Eastern. The sale is advertised through a stewardship agreement with the National Deer Association, which handles solicitation, evaluation, contracting and administration.

The sale is in Kingsley Township, Forest County, along Forest Roads 210, 212, 561 and 561A. Listed sawtimber includes eastern hemlock, black cherry, maple and oak; hardwood pulpwood and hemlock topwood are also included. The stated sale termination date is March 15, 2029.

Bidders must use the Invitation to Bid form and follow its terms. The Forest Service listing says bids must be mailed or emailed to the solicitation contacts and received by the deadline; phone and hand-delivered bids are not accepted. Questions about timber and contract terms go to the contacts in that invitation.
