TENNESSEE — DRYING AND PLANING / NOVEMBER 24 ANNOUNCEMENT

C-Wood targets faster drying and a larger finished-lumber business

C-Wood Lumber plans to invest US$21.5 million in a kiln and dried-lumber planer facility at its existing Collinwood, Tennessee, location. The state’s November 24 announcement projects 25 new jobs, doubling the workforce, and monthly capacity rising from 1.2 million board feet to more than five million.

The new kiln is expected to shorten drying from seven to nine days to 24 hours or less. Those are project targets, not measured results from an operating expansion. C-Wood began as a sawmill in 1983 and shifted exclusively to lumber drying in 1989. The investment concerns drying and finishing lumber, rather than an announced increase in the company’s own log-sawing capacity. The release gives no completion date or new supplier terms.

Equipment & Markets

U.S. / CANADA — EQUIPMENT / NOVEMBER 25 OUTLOOK

Deere sees a flat global forestry-equipment market in 2027

Deere’s November 25 earnings presentation forecasts roughly flat global forestry-equipment industry demand, measured in units, for fiscal 2027. Its outlook for U.S. and Canadian construction equipment is different: flat to five percent growth.

The company’s combined Construction & Forestry division reported fourth-quarter sales of US$3.793 billion, up 12%, while operating profit fell to US$339 million from US$348 million. The division includes construction and other equipment, so that sales increase is not a stand-alone forestry result. For logging-equipment buyers, the useful distinction is between a mixed business’s reported sales and management’s separate, forward-looking forestry-market forecast.

CANADA — NEW BRUNSWICK / PUBLIC PARTICIPATION

Arauco’s St. Stephen air-quality renewal goes to public review

New Brunswick began a 180-day public participation process November 25 for renewal of Arauco Canada’s Class 1 air-quality approval in St. Stephen. The mill makes medium-density fibreboard and particleboard from hardwood chips, softwood shavings and other residual wood fibre. Its current operating approval expires June 30, 2027.

The province’s public-review portal links the facility profile, current approval and notice, with instructions for written comments and questions to the Authorizations Branch. This is review of an existing mill’s operating terms, not an expansion announcement or a new residual-fibre purchasing offer. The portal is also where draft terms and responses to issues raised during consultation will appear.

Under the Radar

ALASKA — FOREST MANAGEMENT / NOVEMBER 24 ADOPTION

Haines gets an adopted forest plan and new land classifications

Alaska adopted the Haines State Forest Resource Management Area Management Plan and Land Classification Order SE-24-001 on November 24. The plan and order affect approximately 280,000 acres of state-owned, state-selected and top-filed lands; that figure is the planning area, not acres opened for timber harvest.

The final plan consists of the public-review draft with the recommended revisions. The new order supersedes earlier land-classification orders within the boundary. The state links those documents and its response to substantive public comments, giving foresters and other land users the adopted planning framework rather than a new timber-sale prospectus.
