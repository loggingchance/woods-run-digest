U.S. — FOREST RESEARCH / NOVEMBER 18 REPORT

Researchers aim to read forest conditions through fungal signals

A US$7.5 million grant will support research into the chemical signals produced by fungi associated with tree roots. The University of California, Santa Barbara reported November 18 that Holly Moeller is leading the work with colleagues at UC Davis and North Carolina State University.

The team will grow tree–fungus partnerships in controlled conditions, examine which environmental cues trigger particular chemicals, and develop sensors to detect them. Researchers envision a small device attached to a root system that could eventually provide readings in a forest. That is a research goal: the announcement does not offer a field-ready monitoring tool or demonstrate a way to diagnose a stand’s condition from fungal signals.

Sunday Feature

Two sisters, a sawmill and the rebuilding of Camp Howard

Sister Krista von Borstel began restoring Camp Howard in Oregon by repairing cabins and replacing roofs. As the camp outgrew its buildings, she and her sister Karen raised support for a dining hall, welcome center and youth center. Seeing the beams and posts going into those projects prompted another idea: mill the camp’s own logs. In Wood-Mizer’s July 31 account, Karen describes operating the sawmill to make roof purlins, panels, sheds and replacement bunk beds. Usable logs become lumber, tops become firewood, and sawdust supplies bedding for the camp’s animals. The camp’s own account says the youth center incorporates timber salvaged from fallen trees on the property. Its dedication took place in September 2024. This is an account of an established rebuilding effort, in which the sisters connected care of the camp’s woods with the places children gather and learn.

CANADA — ONTARIO / APPLICATIONS DUE DECEMBER 13

Ontario habitat grants support land protection and conservation work

The Ontario Land Trust Alliance is accepting 2026–27 Strengthening Species Conservation in Ontario applications through December 13 at 11:59 p.m. Its current call supports land acquisition, conservation easements and on-the-ground work benefiting species at risk.

Applicants must be registered Canadian charities with natural-heritage conservation objectives and OLTA membership or associate status in good standing. Activities must occur in Ontario. The guidelines require at least one dollar of eligible non-provincial matching contributions for each program dollar; cash and eligible in-kind contributions can count. This is a current application opportunity for qualifying organizations, rather than a general grant for individual woodland owners. The organizer’s page links the Foundant application portal.

WASHINGTON — HISTORICAL FIELD INVESTIGATION / 1982 RECORDS

Why the Forest Service Doubted the ‘Bigfoot’ Tracks

The investigators found reasons for skepticism in the mud. Some prints measured about 15 inches, yet the stride stayed roughly four feet even on steeper ground, and the crew’s own boots sank deeper. Wildlife biologist Rodney Johnson concluded that the tracks were inconsistent with the reported heavy biped. A Border Patrol tracker judged them a likely hoax.

The 1982 investigation concerned the Mill Creek watershed in Washington’s Umatilla National Forest. Its photographs and memoranda were posted in the Forest Service’s FOIA reading room October 15, 2026. They document the investigators’ observations and conclusions; they do not establish who made the tracks.
