U.S. & CANADA — WOOD PRODUCTS

ThirdGen adds U.S. log and timber brands in cross-border deal

Calgary-based ThirdGen Timber Group said November 12 that it acquired PrecisionCraft Log & Timber Homes, Riverbend Timber Framing, M.T.N Design and TimberScape from The Riverside Company. The acquired business has design, manufacturing and construction operations in Meridian, Idaho, and Blissfield, Michigan. ThirdGen did not disclose the price.

The deal brings log homes, timber framing and prefabricated panels under the same ownership. ThirdGen said Canadian Timberframes will remain its brand for architects, builders and other business customers. The company did not announce new plant capacity or changes to lumber purchasing.

Trade Policy Watch

  • The U.S. Commerce Department published final subsidy review results November 12 for certain wood mouldings and millwork from China. The 2024 review set rates of 147.88% for three named producers or exporters and 21.39% for non-selected companies in the review. Commerce says it will instruct Customs to use those rates for estimated subsidy-duty deposits on covered entries from November 12; firms outside this review keep their existing applicable rates. Commerce final results, 91 FR 72849

GREAT LAKES — WORKING FORESTS

Fort McCoy awards two timber contracts as next year’s harvest plan goes to review

The Army reported November 12 that foresters at Wisconsin’s Fort McCoy awarded two new timber-sale contracts in October while managing three active sales. In September, three of four advertised tracts sold for a combined $96,357.

Staff also sent the environmental review documents for the fiscal 2027 harvest forward in October. The update gives buyers a sign that another round is in preparation; it does not list tracts, volumes or a bid date.

MIDWEST — MILL TRAINING / NOVEMBER 18

Indiana class follows hardwood grade from the log to the trimmer

The Indiana Hardwood Lumbermen’s Association lists a November 18 grade-sawing class at Pike Lumber in Akron, Indiana. Participants will inspect logs, watch them run through a portable sawmill and trace how decisions at the head saw, resaw, edger and trimmer affect lumber grade. The registration form lists $99 per person for members and $149 for nonmembers.

Under the Radar

WESTERN CANADA — WOOD PRODUCTS RESEARCH

British Columbia cabins put lower-grade boards to work in wall panels

FPInnovations reported November 3 that two demonstration cabins at the University of British Columbia’s Alex Fraser Research Forest use mechanically laminated panels made from three grades of boards. The panels fill the spaces between the posts and beams, giving lower-value, nonstructural boards a role in a finished wall.

The cabins demonstrate a potential outlet for boards a mill might otherwise sell as commodity lumber. The report gives no commercial sales or margin data.
