U.S. — FOREST PRODUCTS / NOVEMBER 18 LEGISLATION

House bill seeks stronger markets for underused wood

Representatives Kim Schrier and GT Thompson introduced the Forest Bioeconomy Act, H.R. 11072, on November 18 to connect Forest Service research more closely with manufacturers and develop uses for wood that has little commercial value. The proposal links forest-management material with product development and mass-timber research.

Schrier’s summary describes a small-business voucher program for access to Forest Service research facilities and an Office of Technology Transfer. These are proposed tools, with no application window open. The House measure has been referred to committees and follows S. 2598, introduced in July 2025. It has not become law; lower housing costs and stronger wood demand remain sponsors’ objectives.

CANADA — BIOMASS MARKETS / NOVEMBER 19 ANNOUNCEMENT

Canada opens a purchasing route for wood-based carbon removal

Canada’s Treasury Board announced 19 competitively established carbon-removal standing offers on November 19. Designated federal departments, agencies and Crown corporations can use them through March 31, 2029, with an extension option.

Wood-related entries include the Pessamit Innu First Nation’s Quebec forest-biomass storage and wildfire-risk project, Ontario’s Green Ventures biochar project and Quebec’s Carbonity biochar plant. The new purchasing route offers a potential demand signal for biomass-based removals. Standing offers do not establish purchase volumes, payments to these suppliers or feedstock contracts, and they are not an open grant or landowner-enrollment program.

CANADA — ALBERTA / SEASONAL FIELD GUIDANCE

Calgary winter-burning guidance calls for checks below the surface

Alberta’s November 18 Calgary Forest Area bulletin says recent snow lowered wildfire danger, but some winter burning projects may still lack suitable conditions. Its guidance calls for at least 15 centimetres of snow cover where winter burns are conducted, with weather and fire-spread potential assessed before ignition.

The bulletin directs continuous monitoring, smoke-warning signs and complete extinguishment before leaving a burn unattended. Piles can retain heat below the surface and reignite days or weeks later. This is regional seasonal guidance for crews managing woody debris, rather than a new universal burning rule.

Under the Radar

CANADA — ONTARIO / HARDWOOD MILL RECOVERY

Five-log study connects purchasing decisions with mill recovery

A November 11 National Hardwood Lumber Association account describes a yield study at Haliburton Forest and Wildlife Reserve in Ontario. Inspectors tested five logs spanning grades and diameters to compare what the mill recovered from different raw material. Those observations offer a mill-specific diagnostic, not an industry benchmark.

The association connects lumber volume and grade with handling costs, processing expenses and the equipment time a log occupies. Its account identifies controllable cuts at the headsaw’s opening face, edger and trimmer. The study concerns recovery and purchasing decisions at an existing operation; no expansion or independently verified savings are announced.
