U.S. — SOUTH AND NORTHWEST / NOVEMBER 4 RESULTS

Rayonier’s first full quarter after merger shows a bigger harvest

Rayonier reported its first full quarter with PotlatchDeltic’s operations on November 4. Its Southern harvest reached 3.02 million tons in the third quarter, up from 1.94 million a year earlier. Former PotlatchDeltic timberlands contributed about 1.4 million tons. Northwest harvest rose to 571,000 tons from 210,000, also chiefly because of the added acreage.

Average delivered pine pulpwood prices in Rayonier’s Southern segment fell to US$31.14 a ton from US$36.47 a year earlier. The company attributed that change to both weaker pulpwood markets and a different geographic mix. The year-earlier quarter covers Rayonier alone, so the volume gains measure a larger combined business.

Rayonier also closed two previously announced transactions with Resource Management Service: a US$145 million sale of about 36,000 acres in Washington and a US$146 million purchase of about 57,000 acres in Texas and Alabama.

Under the Radar

Kansas field day links woodland care with small-scale milling

A November 2 KCLY report revisits the Kansas Forestry Association's October 22 field day at Woodland Ranch near Courtland. Landowners saw sawmill and post-peeler demonstrations alongside sessions on oak management, young tree plantations, wildlife habitat and streambank stabilization.

Kansas Forest Service district forester Thad Rhodes said management can improve established woodlands, wildlife habitat and water quality. He pointed to windbreaks and streamside buffers as ways to protect the land. The event paired those stewardship topics with practical wood utilization.

U.S. — WEST / NOVEMBER 6 RESEARCH SUMMARY

Low snow improved some logging access and cut it elsewhere

A Forest Service research summary published November 6 describes how the West’s 2026 snow drought changed field operations. Managers in a survey and focus groups generally reported easier access to project sites. In places where winter logging depends on frozen ground or sufficient snow cover, work was limited instead.

The same report describes difficulties with pile burning where snow usually helps contain fire. It records managers’ observations across the West; it does not estimate regional timber volume gained or lost.

Action Detector

Nebraska's Central Platte opens spring conservation-tree orders

Central Platte Natural Resources District is accepting conservation-tree and shrub orders for spring 2027. Seedlings cost US$31.25 per bundle of 25. Orders placed by December 31 receive a 10% discount on trees, weed barrier and planting services; the final ordering deadline is April 1, 2027.

Qualifying orders of at least 200 trees may receive cost-share assistance for trees and weed barrier. The district also offers planting services and smaller mixed-species packages. These are Central Platte's program terms; landowners elsewhere should check their own district's prices and eligibility.
