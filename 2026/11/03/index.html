Searsmont welcomes Lily Robbins home after months of recovery

Lily Robbins returned to Searsmont, Maine, on October 30 after five and a half months at Massachusetts General Hospital. First responders escorted her home, where family, friends and neighbors gathered at the fire station to welcome her.

Robbins, a recent University of Maine graduate and volunteer EMT, has been recovering from injuries sustained in the May 15 fire and explosion at her family’s mill, Robbins Lumber. For readers who would like to help with recovery expenses, a GoFundMe organized on her behalf is linked below.

CANADA — BRITISH COLUMBIA / OCTOBER 30 CLOSING

Gillfor completes its purchase of Teal-Jones coastal operations

Gillfor Manufacturing has completed its acquisition of Teal-Jones’ Surrey, Sumas and Tree Farm Licence 46 operations. The October 30 company statement confirms that Gillfor is now the sole owner, following a September 18 British Columbia Supreme Court reverse vesting order.

The closing moves the transaction beyond the earlier court-approval stage. Gillfor says the Teal-Jones operating name and contact personnel remain unchanged, and it is working with customers, suppliers and service providers to restore normal business terms and regular services.

Excluded pre-closing claims and liabilities remain with the Companies’ Creditors Arrangement Act estate. The company directs claims, invoices and issues through October 30 to court-appointed monitor Ernst & Young, rather than treating them as obligations of the acquired business.

Gillfor says the transaction preserves hundreds of jobs and combines sawmilling, value-added manufacturing and distribution capabilities in western redcedar, hemlock and Douglas-fir. The announcement does not provide a mill-by-mill production timetable.

CANADA — NEWFOUNDLAND AND LABRADOR / APPLICATIONS EXTENDED

Communities get until November 30 to seek wildfire-planning support

Newfoundland and Labrador has extended applications for its Community Wildfire Prevention and Mitigation Program to November 30. Forestry, Agriculture and Lands Minister Pleaman Forsey confirmed the extension in a November 2 statement to the House of Assembly.

The program helps communities identify and reduce wildfire risk and plan for emergencies. The minister encouraged communities to consult the department or contact FireSmart@gov.nl.ca for application information.

The announcement concerns community applications, rather than an individual homeowner rebate or an awarded fuels-treatment contract. It comes after the official forest-fire season, with the province urging preparations for the next season to continue.

CANADA — QUEBEC / TROPICAL FOREST RESEARCH

Open drone workflow brings individual tree crowns into focus

A research team led by Université de Montréal and Mila professor Étienne Laliberté is sharing an open-source workflow for close-up canopy photography. Mila’s October 28 account describes Harpia, a Python library that automates drone missions for the Kanopia forest-monitoring project.

The system first maps a forest from above, identifies individual crowns and then programs return flights for detailed images of leaves, flowers and fruits. These photographs support botanical identification and training data for species-recognition models.

The team reports photographing more than 24,000 canopy trees across Panama, Ecuador and Brazil. Pilot work on Panama’s Barro Colorado Island draws on its extensive botanical records to check identifications.

The paper appeared October 19 in Methods in Ecology and Evolution. This is a tropical-canopy research workflow; the reporting does not establish a ready-made replacement for North American field inventories or verified classification accuracy in other forest types.
