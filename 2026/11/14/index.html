U.S. SOUTH — WOOD FIBRE

Lenzing puts $31 million into specialty wood fibre in Alabama

Lenzing announced November 12 that it will invest US$31 million at its Mobile lyocell plant. The project expands its ability to make short-cut, wood-based fibres for nonwoven materials, giving the plant more flexibility to tailor fibre to customer specifications.

The Alabama work is part of a €50 million nonwovens investment that also includes previously announced work in Austria. Lenzing did not give a completion date, added tonnage or a new pulp purchasing commitment.

Forestry & Wood Products Profile

Eric Fritch found room for a mill that cuts to order

Eric Fritch grew up working at his family’s Washington sawmill, driving a log truck, making deliveries and learning the mill floor. After a 1996 fire, he returned to a business that still had a working planer and was doing custom work. In a September 2024 interview with equipment supplier Wood-Mizer, Fritch recalled rebuilding around jobs that called for unusual dimensions or tight turnaround. He said the mill held a substantial log inventory so he knew what orders he could promise, while keeping little finished lumber on hand because the output was already sold. When independent distributors had trouble getting fencing during the pandemic, the mill adapted its equipment to cut cedar fencing as well as the larger pieces it normally made.

Working Forests

WESTERN CANADA — WORKING FORESTS

Cariboo Chilcotin forum looks for closer links between timber suppliers and local mills

Williams Lake’s November 12 recap of its November 6 forestry forum says participants called for closer ties between timber licensees and local manufacturers. The meeting brought together First Nations, local governments and industry and included visits to two timber-frame businesses.

Central Chilcotin Rehabilitation presented work on recovering fibre from fire-affected trees. The city says participants also proposed reviewing timber pricing and appraisal to help facilitate direct sales and quicker salvage. These were discussion priorities; the forum did not change sale or appraisal rules.

U.S. SOUTH — BIDS DUE NOVEMBER 24

Sand Hills offers a 49-acre pine and hardwood sale

South Carolina’s Forestry Commission will open bids for sale SHSF-5387-ADV-TS-27 at 10 a.m. Eastern on November 24. The 49-acre Chesterfield County tract has an estimated 6,800 tons of pine and 550 tons of hardwood. Bidders must submit a separate blended price per ton for each group and make their own volume estimates.

The prospectus lists a $5,000 performance bond, a $25,000 advance and 12 months to harvest from contract signing. It includes maps and crew training requirements.

PACIFIC NORTHWEST — FOREST MANAGEMENT

Bureau of Land Management posts sixth Last Chance decision

The Bureau of Land Management’s Grants Pass Field Office announced November 13 that it had issued its sixth timber-sale decision under the Last Chance Forest Management Project in Josephine County, Oregon. The project page records the signing and links the decision documents. The announcement itself gives no bid opening or advertised volume.
