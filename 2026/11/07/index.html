CANADA — NOVEMBER 5 FEDERAL BUDGET

Canada’s budget proposes C$6.5 billion to retool forestry

Canada’s November 5 federal budget proposes C$6.5 billion in forest-sector support over six years beginning in 2027–28. The largest piece is C$4 billion on a cash basis through Export Development Canada’s Canada Account for a new Forestry Sector Accelerator. It would back large new facilities and major conversions, including biorefineries and biofuel plants.

Another C$2 billion would go to Natural Resources Canada for mill and equipment modernization, higher-value production, and research and demonstration projects. A separate C$500 million would support wood-fibre supply chains, forest stewardship and new practices with provinces, territories, Indigenous partners and industry.

These are budget proposals with a future start date. The Forest Products Association of Canada welcomed the direction while calling for quick implementation and awaiting the full strategy. The budget sets out investment purposes; it does not announce an open application window.

Forestry & Wood Products Profile

Ken Breon and the Lumber Capital family

Ken Breon started in residential tree removal in Pennsylvania. Finding uses for the wood led him into firewood, then into logging and milling. In Wood-Mizer’s April 2026 Wood Life interview, Ken and daughters Emerald and Jade describe how that work grew into a yard with milling, kiln drying and retail sales. The family now divides the work between two businesses: Ken runs Ironwood Acres Timber Harvest, while Teri and their daughters operate Lumber Capital Log Yard in Cogan Station. The daughters took on mill work and developed the family’s YouTube channel, showing how the wood is cut and processed. The businesses grew from a practical question: what else could they make from the trees Ken was already taking down?

U.S. — MAINE / NOVEMBER 6 AWARD

Maine honors logging brothers for stewardship of working forests

Will and Don Cole of Sidney, who operate the family logging business Trees Ltd., received Maine’s 2026 Austin H. Wilkins Forest Stewardship Award. The state’s November 6 announcement highlighted their work on public and private woodlands across central and southern Maine.

It singled out harvests at Maine TREE’s Holt Research Forest in Arrowsic, where the brothers’ work has helped demonstrate forest management to landowners and other visitors. Maine TREE and the state agriculture and forestry department present the award together.

U.S. WEST — NOVEMBER 6 FOREST SERVICE REPORT

Low snow helped some winter logging jobs and limited others

A new Forest Service summary of the 2026 western snow drought describes a split in winter operations. Staff reported easier access to some logging and management sites, while other jobs were limited because they needed frozen ground or more snow cover.

Effects on pile burning also varied. Some higher-elevation areas could reach piles while still having enough snow for burning, but prolonged snow-free periods restricted opportunities in drier parts of the Rockies. Earlier broadcast-burn windows were useful only where plans and crews were ready.

The findings are preliminary observations from 92 questionnaire responses across 12 western states and follow-up focus groups. They show why easier road access alone does not tell a manager whether a winter operation can proceed.

U.S. — WASHINGTON / NOVEMBER 17 PUBLIC FORUM

Cle Elum Ridge opens its draft community forest plan to feedback

The Nature Conservancy announced that it acquired about 2,600 acres in Kittitas County and established the Cle Elum Ridge Community Forest. The conservation group describes the site as a working forest that will remain protected from development and support public recreation, watershed function and habitat.

Residents can review the draft management plan and offer feedback at a November 17 forum, 5:30–7 p.m. Pacific, at the UKC Senior Center, 719 E Third Street in Cle Elum. The Nature Conservancy and Yakama Nation describe a future transfer to the Nation as a long-term goal; ownership has not yet transferred.
