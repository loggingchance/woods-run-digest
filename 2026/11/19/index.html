U.S. WEST — CALIFORNIA / NOVEMBER 17 ACQUISITION

Sierra Pacific plans to restart Burney’s biomass plant

Sierra Pacific Industries has acquired the Burney Forest Products biomass power plant assets in California and plans to refurbish the facility for a 2027 restart. The company says it will integrate the plant into its existing Burney operations and add about 20 permanent jobs once it is operating.

The plant uses sawmill residuals and low-value forest wood to generate electricity and steam for lumber drying. Its capacity is up to 30 megawatts. The announcement establishes a planned return of this wood outlet, without giving a restart month, purchase volumes or new supplier terms.

CANADA — DOMESTIC WOOD MARKETS / INDUSTRY ADVOCACY

Canadian wood groups join a push to remove housing barriers

The Forest Products Association of Canada and Canadian Wood Council have joined the Homes and Growth Coalition, which is developing proposals to get more housing built. Their November 9 announcement, posted November 10, connects domestic wood use and factory-built construction with changes to investment, taxes and fees, regulation and housing data.

The groups want building and approval rules that make Canadian manufacturing capacity easier to use. This is an industry policy agenda, with no enacted reforms or new wood orders announced.

CANADA — QUEBEC / COMMENTS THROUGH DECEMBER 15

Mauricie opens windthrow salvage plans to public comment

Quebec is consulting on recovery of trees blown down or damaged by summer 2026 winds in four Mauricie public-forest management units: 041-51, 042-51, 043-51 and 043-52. The proposed plan maps possible harvest areas and regeneration work where needed. Comments are open through December 15.

The interactive map also accepts comments, but will be unavailable from November 25 at 6:30 p.m. through November 29; service is expected to resume November 30. A December 3 information session requires registration by the previous day. This is a planning consultation, not an advertised harvest contract.

Under the Radar

U.S. WEST — CALIFORNIA / NOVEMBER CASE STUDY

Forestry training support reaches beyond the classroom

A November Berkeley case study of California’s Resilient Careers in Forestry program identifies transportation, equipment and employer connections as important supports for trainees. The program helps rural participants with travel costs and supplies such as wildland fire boots, while partner organizations provide access to specialized training equipment.

The report counts 2,426 people enrolled in at least one program—not graduates or verified job placements. Its findings draw on 2026 interviews and communications with program leadership. The program began in 2022 and continues through May 2027; the case study is not a new funding or enrollment announcement.
