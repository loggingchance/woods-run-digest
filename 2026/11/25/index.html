U.S. — FOREST OPERATIONS / NOVEMBER 24 RESEARCH BRIEF

SlashScan brings slash-pile measurement into the field

Forest Service and Oregon State University researchers describe an iPhone and iPad app that estimates slash-pile volume and records pile locations while the operator walks around the material. The agency’s November 24 brief says SlashScan performs scanning, reconstruction and calculations on the device, without Wi-Fi or separate equipment. It currently requires an iOS device with lidar; Android is not yet available.

The underlying study compared 14 piles with drone-based lidar measurements and found no statistically significant difference between the methods’ volume estimates. That is a limited validation, rather than proof of accuracy for every pile or site. Knowing where material sits and roughly how much is there can support biomass inventories, hauling estimates and disposal planning; the measurement does not establish a buyer or a profitable outlet.

CANADA — PANEL SUPPLY / NOVEMBER 23 DECISION

Canada’s plywood decision leaves the injury inquiry open

Canada Border Services Agency made a final dumping determination on Chinese decorative and other non-structural plywood November 23. It ended the subsidy investigation for nine named exporters whose subsidies were insignificant, while making a final subsidy determination for the remaining exporters.

The Canadian International Trade Tribunal’s injury inquiry continues, with a decision due by December 22. The notice says provisional duties continue until that decision; ending one exporter’s subsidy investigation is not blanket relief from the dumping case. For Canadian panel buyers and manufacturers, the relevant checks remain the product definition and exporter-specific treatment. The agency cautions that its final dumping margins are not the duty rates for future shipments.

ALASKA — ROAD ACCESS / COMMENTS THROUGH JANUARY 15

Alaska extends comments on an existing logging-road easement

Alaska’s November 23 amended notice extends written comments on proposed public-access easement ADL 234961 to January 15, 2027. The Division of Forestry & Fire Protection seeks an indefinite easement for an existing gravel logging-road network near Houston and Meadow Lakes, including W. Zero Lake Road.

The stated purposes include road maintenance for emergency response, commercial timber operations and firewood cutting, alongside recreation, subsistence and mushing uses. This is an application under review, not an approved route or a timber-sale offer. The notice links the development plan and map and identifies the adjudicator for questions; comments must reach the Southcentral Regional Land Office by the deadline.

Under the Radar

CALIFORNIA — WORKING-FOREST RESEARCH / NOVEMBER 24 PAPER

Flatwoods sets up a long-term test of thinning and fire

A new Journal of Forestry paper describes how northern California’s Flatwoods Research Forest is allocating mature stands among fire and non-fire fuel treatments, different canopy patterns and stocking levels. Randomized, replicated treatments and permanent monitoring plots are intended to test approaches to drought and severe-fire risk while retaining wood production.

This is the experimental framework, not completed evidence that one treatment wins. Canopy gaps also create space for reforestation studies. The authors excluded permanently untreated reserves because of space constraints and working-forest objectives, a design choice that limits some comparisons. The practical contribution is a structure for following treatment outcomes over time under regional operating conditions.

Wednesday Podcast Pick

The Rise and Fall of Black Cherry

SilviCast

This November 1 episode brings research ecologist Alex Royo and University of Kentucky forest-management researcher Lance Vickers into a discussion of declining black-cherry regeneration, growth and survival in the Allegheny hardwood region. The Wisconsin Forestry Center’s SilviCast examines the ecological and social influences behind those changes and what they mean for silvicultural decisions. A useful, dated discussion for foresters managing future hardwood stands; it is not a current timber-price report.
