NORTHEAST — VERMONT / BUSINESS FUNDING

Vermont’s business-improvement grant window opens today

Vermont’s Working Lands Business Enhancement Grant is scheduled to open today, October 9. The program page lists awards of $10,000–$50,000, no required match, and a December 10 deadline at 2:00 p.m. Vermont time. Equipment, infrastructure, market development, research and development, and workforce development are eligible project categories; applicant and project restrictions still apply.

The applicant webinar is October 14, 11 a.m.–noon. WebGrants registration can take two business days. The page reviewed still labels some linked documents as samples from the prior round; use the new request for applications when posted rather than assuming a sample governs this intake.

Our business interpretation: start with the constraint that costs the company money, then describe the proposed change and how its result will be measured. A project that saves two crew-hours each week needs a credible baseline, a way to record the improvement, and a cost estimate that includes implementation. A grant can help pay for that change, but the operating case should stand on its own. Keep the requested scope tight enough that the company can complete and use it.

Watch: the new RFA, eligibility, registration and a measurable operating result.

Mills & Markets

CANADA — WESTERN FIBRE / MARKETS

Fewer pulp buyers are changing the value of logs and residual chips

Forisk’s September 28 review of third-quarter 2026 markets reports British Columbia Interior delivered pulplog prices about 13% below their 2025 levels, after three consecutive quarterly declines. Coastal residual-chip prices fell roughly 15% in a quarter, while Alberta SPF chip prices edged higher. Forisk links the pressure to pulp-mill closures and curtailments that remove fibre buyers.

These are regional market movements, not a posted price for a particular truckload. The contrast between British Columbia and Alberta is useful: the same product can face different buyer availability and haul economics. For a sawmill or harvesting business, ask which customers will take each residual product, how much they will accept, and what the delivery actually costs before counting that product’s sales contribution.

Watch: buyer availability and net value after hauling, not just the quoted price.

CANADA / U.S. — MERCER / MILL FINANCE

Mercer’s debt negotiations warrant attention without assuming a shutdown

Mercer International’s October 2 SEC disclosure says it is negotiating a recapitalization and capital infusion with noteholders holding more than 75% of the relevant outstanding notes. It elected to use a 30-day grace period for an approximately US$25.8 million interest payment due October 1. Mercer says using that period is not an event of default under the indenture.

The same filing says pulp, lumber and mass-timber operations continue in the ordinary course, including payments to suppliers, contractors and employees. It gives no assurance that a definitive agreement will be reached or when. Those statements are the company’s dated disclosure, not independent verification of every payment today. Our supplier-side reading is to monitor subsequent company notices and normal account performance while keeping financial negotiations distinct from an announced operating curtailment.

Watch: a definitive agreement and subsequent operating or payment notices.

SOUTH — FLORIDA / DEADLINE TODAY

Last day for the 2026 southern pine-beetle prevention intake

Florida’s published application period ends today, October 9. Eligible non-industrial private forest owners in 44 northern counties may request support for first pulpwood thinning, prescribed burning, longleaf or slash pine planting, and mechanical underbrush treatment. Requests are limited to $10,000 and up to two approved practices; minimum area is generally 10 acres, or 5 acres for first thinning.

The state requires a verified substitute W-9 and its printout with the application materials. The agency says applications received or postmarked after the period will not be considered or receive lowest priority for remaining funds. Contact the county forester promptly to resolve submission details; do not treat a late application as equally eligible for the regular ranking.

WEST — MONTANA / OCTOBER 13 BID

Case for Potomac offers 8,020 tons, with seasonal access costs to examine

Montana DNRC’s Case for Potomac sale offers approximately 8,020 tons of mixed-conifer sawlogs. Sealed bids open Tuesday, October 13, at 2 p.m. Mountain at 2705 Spurgin Road in Missoula. The notice requires a $2,496 bid deposit and recommends confirming that the bid has arrived before opening.

The prospectus anticipates seasonal non-operable conditions from mid-October through November and March through mid-June. It also specifies road maintenance and herbicide obligations, with harvesting completed by July 31, 2029. Non-sawlog material is optional to haul at $1 per ton. This is a near-term bidding decision with a longer operating horizon: price the usable work windows and all contract obligations. The package warns that its volumes and cost estimates are not guarantees.

NORTHEAST — FOUR-STATE REGION / OCTOBER 16

Forest Economy applications are due next week—but only by invitation

The Northern Border Regional Commission’s current Forest Economy overview lists fall full applications due before 5 p.m. on October 16; the document labels that time EST. Submit early and confirm the portal cutoff with the program office. The required pre-application period has already ended, and full applications are accepted only from entities expressly invited to apply.

Eligible applicants include qualifying nonprofits, governments and federally recognized tribes; for-profit businesses cannot apply directly. Awards can reach $1 million. The program covers portions of Maine, New Hampshire and New York, and all Vermont counties. It supports forest-economy infrastructure, workforce work and technical assistance, among other purposes. For invited applicants, the immediate job is to finish a ready project and its supporting commitments. For others, contact NBRC about a future round rather than beginning an ineligible last-minute application.

Under the Radar

NATIONAL / MIDWEST — CONSERVATION INCOME

USDA begins $1.8 billion in annual CRP payments

USDA’s October 8 announcement says approximately $1.8 billion in annual Conservation Reserve Program and Grassland CRP rental payments are being issued for 2026. It projects 26.8 million enrolled acres for fiscal 2027 after accepting 2.2 million acres through this year’s signups. Iowa, Illinois, Minnesota, South Dakota and Missouri are the top five states for 2026 payments.

This is a payment announcement for participating landowners, not a newly opened grant competition. For mixed farm-and-forest ownerships, conservation payments are a distinct revenue line with their own enrolled acres and obligations. Check an existing contract and the local FSA office before treating a general conservation headline as income available to an unenrolled woodland.

WEST / ALASKA — FOREST DATA / NEW TOOL

A new dashboard separates national-forest timber sold from timber cut

The Forest Service’s Pacific Northwest Research Station released a cut-and-sold dashboard on October 6. It provides annual volume and value for sawtimber and other wood products by ranger district in western national forests and Alaska, drawing from the Timber Information Manager. Users can filter the maps and tables and download CSV data.

Our practical use: compare sale volume with harvested volume before calling a supply trend a change in actual production. The two measures can move differently because contracts are purchased and harvested on different schedules. Review the years, product definitions and units in an export before comparing districts. This is an annual historical analysis tool, not a list of currently open bids.

WEST — ALASKA / TIMBER ACCESS

Roadless-rule headlines still require a sale-by-sale supply check

KTOO’s October 8 reporting describes competing views after the public-comment period on the proposed Roadless Rule rescission closed. An Alaska timber-industry representative told KTOO that lifting the rule would not immediately produce new timber sales. The report also notes the separate Tongass forest-plan revision.

The federal proposal itself says rescission would not authorize specific ground-disturbing projects; future road or harvest proposals would need site-specific review and consistency with the governing forest plan. Our business reading: track actual sale preparation, access and operability before translating a policy proposal into available logs. A change in planning flexibility and a deliverable timber supply are different stages of the process.

FOREST BUSINESS SCHOOL RESOURCE

Put a crew briefing behind the emergency plan

The free Woods-Ready Safety Plan app helps loggers and foresters prepare a site-specific emergency response plan. It was built for the Northeastern Loggers’ Association with grant funding from the National Timber Harvesting and Transportation Safety Foundation.

Before starting the next job, prepare the plan and walk the crew through the location, responder directions and contacts. A plan becomes more useful when the people on site know where it is and how to use it. Revisit the briefing when the job location, access or crew changes.
