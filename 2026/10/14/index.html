CANADA — QUEBEC / SAWMILL RESTART

Petit Paris plans a smaller restart while protecting its wood supply

Le Réveil reports that the Petit Paris sawmill in Saint-Ludger-de-Milot plans to restart part of its production in mid-October with about 20 employees, compared with roughly 80 before its 2024 shutdown. The original report was published October 11 and updated October 12. It does not give a production-capacity figure or confirm that the restart is complete.

Mayor Marc Laliberté tells the newspaper that resuming operations is necessary to preserve the mill’s supply guarantee. A longer-term plan is being developed. The plant includes sawing, drying and planing operations, but the report does not specify how much of each process will return immediately.

Our supplier interpretation: a limited restart may preserve an industrial foothold without restoring the former purchasing volume. Establish which logs the mill will accept, its delivery schedule and payment terms. Size the opportunity around actual orders, and keep another outlet available while the operation settles into its new scale.

Watch: actual startup, purchasing specifications and the longer-term operating plan.

CANADA — ONTARIO / WOOD COMPONENTS

Atlas completes commissioning; production performance is the next test

Atlas Engineered Products announced October 8 that commissioning is complete at its new 42,000-square-foot facility in Clinton, Ontario. Training and testing are underway on sellable roof-truss production runs. The company describes the installation as Canada’s first robotic roof-truss manufacturing line and acknowledges support from the federal IFIT program.

The release identifies a completed installation and commissioning milestone. It does not report realized unit costs, throughput or productivity gains. Management’s expectations for greater capacity and operating efficiency remain expectations until operating results demonstrate them.

Our production interpretation: evaluate an automated line through saleable output and its effect on the whole order flow. Cutting, component preparation, changeovers, maintenance, quality checks and shipping still influence what reaches the customer. For a lumber supplier, the useful questions concern input specifications, inventory replenishment and the consistency of orders as testing gives way to routine production.

Watch: saleable output, changeovers and actual operating costs.

WEST — OREGON / WASHINGTON / WYOMING / IDAHO

BLM reports timber sold, with another October offering plan

BLM’s October 7 quarterly release reports $29.8 million in timber-sale revenue and about 121 million board feet sold across 5,300 acres during July–September 2026 in Oregon, Washington, Wyoming and Idaho. The agency also describes plans for nine October sales offering about 46 million board feet across more than 1,200 acres.

These figures describe sales and an offering plan. They do not measure the amount already harvested or delivered to mills. The agency’s timber-sale page routes buyers to regional plans, notices and prospectuses, which provide the specific dates and conditions needed for an individual bid.

Our procurement interpretation: work from the sale-level documents to a usable supply schedule. Species, access, contract timing and delivered costs determine whether advertised volume fits an operation. A regional total establishes the scale of the program; it cannot establish a particular mill’s near-term intake or a contractor’s available margin.

Watch: individual bid notices and the difference between sold and delivered timber.

MIDWEST — WISCONSIN / BIDS DUE TOMORROW

Iron County’s thirteen timber tracts close October 15 at 8:30 a.m.

Iron County’s Fall 2026 prospectus accepts sealed bids for thirteen timber tracts until October 15 at 8:30 a.m. Hurley time, with opening at 9 a.m. Bids must reach the Forestry Department Office at 607 Third Avenue North, Suite 2, Hurley. Wisconsin DNR’s current county-sale listing independently confirms the October 15 date.

The county requires its bid form and a separate sealed envelope for each tract. A deposit of at least 10% of the total bid accompanies the offer; the prospectus specifies checks or money orders and excludes letters of credit and surety bonds for that bid deposit. Review the complete notice for payment, training, insurance and tract-specific conditions.

Our immediate priority: verify receipt and the correct deposit while there is still time to resolve a submission problem. Carry the tract’s product outlets, ground conditions and operating restrictions into the cost estimate. Tomorrow’s opening rewards a prepared estimate and complete bid, rather than a rushed expansion of the bidding plan.

SOUTH — SOUTH CAROLINA / OCTOBER 27 BIDS

Sand Hills offers separate pine and hardwood prices per ton

South Carolina Forestry Commission’s current Sand Hills sale SHSF-5378-ADV-TS-27 opens October 27 at 10 a.m. South Carolina time. The prospectus estimates about 7,600 tons of pine and 500 tons of hardwood on roughly 72 acres in Darlington County. Bids may be mailed, delivered or emailed as instructed in the notice.

The bid format requires one blended price per ton for pine products and another for hardwood products. The notice specifies a $5,000 performance bond, a $25,000 advance and twelve months from contract signing to complete harvesting. Buyers must develop their own volume estimates and review the road, insurance, training and pre-logging requirements.

Our bidding interpretation: cost each product stream around its actual destinations, then add the cash tied up before production begins. An advance changes the financing requirement even when the sale has attractive delivered margins. Confirm the submission route and reference the exact sale number.

WEST — CALIFORNIA / SITE VISIT TOMORROW

Sunland fuels project offers an October 15 field look

The National Forest Foundation’s current procurement page lists an optional October 15 site visit for the Sunland Fuels Reduction project on the Angeles National Forest. The meeting is at 10:30 a.m. local time at the Big Tujunga Canyon Road and Graveyard Truck Trail turnout near Tujunga. NFF asks interested participants to notify it in advance.

The project covers approximately 146 acres and describes roadside hazardous-fuel reduction along part of Road 2N86, with wider treatment in two areas. Responses are due October 29. Use the page’s current materials and listed contact for the treatment specifications and submission requirements.

Our field-review interpretation: use the visit to connect the written treatment scope to access, vegetation, disposal and production constraints. This is service work whose economics depend on what must be treated and how the work can be completed. Confirm material handling requirements before counting on revenue from removed wood.

Under the Radar

NORTHEAST — VERMONT / FOREST-BLOCK RULE DEVELOPMENT

Vermont separates the new forest criterion from permit jurisdiction

An October 13 Notch FM report revisits the Land Use Review Board’s September 30 Burke session on forest blocks and habitat connectors. The board’s official page says Criterion 8(C) will apply to Act 250 applications filed on or after January 1, 2028. The criterion itself does not determine when or where an Act 250 permit is required.

The current timeline places proposed-rule drafting in October–December 2026, public input in January 2027 and hearings in May 2027. The board expressly says milestones may change. This is rule development for a future review standard, rather than a new rule already being applied to every forest ownership.

Our landowner interpretation: separate permit jurisdiction from the standards used to review a project that requires a permit. Owners considering development can follow the draft definitions and mitigation provisions while retaining the property’s actual management and access needs in their planning. Use the board’s materials to evaluate proposed wording as it becomes available.

NATIONAL — BIOMASS MARKETS / JULY DATA

Pellet sales and feedstock purchases answer different market questions

EIA’s Monthly Densified Biomass Fuel Report, released October 8, covers July 2026. Its monthly respondents bought 0.96 million tons of feedstock, produced 0.96 million tons of fuel and sold 1.0 million tons. EIA cautions that feedstock purchases do not directly correspond to production in the same reporting period.

July domestic sales were 0.12 million tons, averaging $243.39 per ton; exports were 0.88 million tons, averaging $210.91. Those are fuel-product sales averages, not prices paid for logging residues or delivered roundwood. The report’s feedstock tables distinguish roundwood, sawmill residue, manufacturing residue and other residuals.

Our market interpretation: identify both the material and the transaction before using an average price in a wood-supply estimate. A pellet selling price incorporates processing and a different product specification. Evaluate the local buyer’s accepted feedstock, delivered quote and hauling cost rather than working backward from a national pellet-sales average.

FOREST BUSINESS SCHOOL RESOURCE

Keep the job’s emergency plan connected to its access

The free Woods-Ready Safety Plan app helps loggers and foresters prepare a site-specific emergency response plan. It was built for the Northeastern Loggers’ Association with grant funding from the National Timber Harvesting and Transportation Safety Foundation.

Before moving to another tract, check responder directions against the route the crew is actually using. A different landing, locked gate or seasonal road closure can change the approach. Review the location and contacts with the crew, and make sure the plan is available where people can find it. The useful plan is the one that describes today’s work site clearly.
