SOUTH — ARKANSAS / OCTOBER 27 ANNOUNCEMENT

Canfor’s Arkansas expansion links more lumber output with rail access

Canfor Southern Pine is investing approximately US$96 million in modernization and logistics at Iron Mountain in El Dorado, Arkansas. The October 27 announcement says the projects are expected to more than double annual net production to about 220 million board feet and add approximately 75 facility and logistics jobs.

The planer upgrade finished in the second quarter of 2025. Sawmill and log-yard work is expected to finish in the fourth quarter of 2026. A transload facility built earlier this year serves Iron Mountain and nearby Urbana, moving lumber between trucks and railcars to reach North American customers.

The figures describe a phased project, not an entirely new investment beginning this week. Iron Mountain is expected to employ about 150 people directly when completed; the release does not identify a separate retained-jobs total.

APPALACHIANS — FOREST RESEARCH / OCTOBER 27 REPORT

Study tests how storms followed by drought change wildfire fuels

North Carolina State University and partners are beginning a three-year study of how rapid wet-to-dry weather shifts affect wildfire risk in the Blue Ridge region. The October 27 announcement describes field measurements of fuel loads and moisture loss, fuel mapping and scenario modelling.

The researchers hypothesize that storm-damaged branches and leaves can dry during subsequent drought, increasing available fuel. They will test that mechanism with Mississippi State University, the North Carolina State Climate Office and the U.S. Forest Service.

The aim is to help managers identify vulnerable areas and evaluate risk-reduction strategies. This is a research question being investigated, rather than a completed finding that every storm-damaged stand will face greater fire risk.

CANADA — WORKFORCE / OCTOBER 22 PROGRAM LAUNCH

Work-sharing changes give employers more scheduling flexibility

Canada’s Workforce Retention and Retraining Program allows eligible tariff-affected employers to distribute reduced hours more flexibly, temporarily removing the equal-sharing requirement. The October 22 federal launch also offers up to C$1,000 per participating employee for eligible training and administration costs.

Successive approved agreements can provide up to 152 cumulative weeks of support; that is not an automatic single agreement. Employers must offer training opportunities for at least 40% of the agreement period, with employees training during non-working hours.

Existing Work-Sharing and Worker Retention Grant participants transition automatically. New applicants use Grants and Contributions Online Services. The program covers eligible businesses across sectors through March 31, 2028; its current application and eligibility rules govern access.

NORTHEAST — MAINE / WORKSHOP OCTOBER 29

Maine community-forestry grants require tomorrow’s workshop

Maine’s 2026 Project Canopy call requires prospective applicants to attend a virtual workshop October 29, from 1–3 p.m. The state lists December 17 at 11:59 p.m. as the application deadline and requires a 50% cash or in-kind match.

The US$390,000 pool includes community-forestry assistance and emerald ash borer mitigation. Award ceilings are US$20,000 for planning and education, US$15,000 for planting and maintenance, and US$20,000 for ash-borer mitigation.

This is community-forestry project assistance, rather than a general logging-equipment grant. The current program page links workshop registration and category guidelines; the workshop is a prerequisite even though the application deadline is later.

Under the Radar

FOREST HEALTH — IDENTIFICATION / OCTOBER 27 REPORT

Expanded beetle database separates pest identity from reputation

The University of Florida Forest Entomology Lab’s Bark and Ambrosia Beetles of the World database has released version 2.0. Its developers report 7,990 species, 15,778 specimen records and 32,403 images, with searches by host, taxonomy and geography.

In their October 27 account, the researchers distinguish the relatively few destructive pests from the many species associated with unhealthy or dying trees. The updated search tools, captions and mobile interface support identification and biosecurity work; a database match alone does not establish a treatment prescription.
