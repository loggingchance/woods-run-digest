CANADA — QUÉBEC / MILL OWNERSHIP

Local coalition pursues the purchase of Témiscaming’s mill

A coalition of local RYAM managers, unionized workers and First Nations is pursuing the purchase of the Témiscaming mill, Unifor announced October 9. The union says the group wants to develop a locally driven solution and examine what is needed to restart operations. The announcement comes from a participant supporting the initiative; it does not report a completed transaction or give a restart date.

The coalition says further details will follow as discussions progress. Unifor invokes the community’s 1973 purchase and reopening of the former Canadian International Paper mill under the Tembec name. That history explains the local ownership ambition, but it does not establish financing or operating arrangements for this effort.

Our supplier-side interpretation: track this as a developing ownership proposal. A useful next update would identify the transaction’s status, restart requirements and a purchasing schedule. Before building a crew or trucking budget around renewed mill demand, seek confirmed delivery arrangements and account terms. Local support matters; an operating plan still has to translate it into orders that suppliers can fulfill.

Watch: transaction details, restart requirements and confirmed purchasing arrangements.

Mills & Markets

U.S. — HARDWOODS / MILL CAPACITY

New closure tracking shows how much the hardwood buyer network has changed

Fordaq’s October 9 account of September’s NHLA convention reports that Hardwood Review identified 151 publicly announced U.S. hardwood sawmill and yard closures since the start of 2023, plus 95 secondary-manufacturing closures. Dan Meyer’s count uses auction listings, news reports and industry information. It is a minimum of publicly identified closures, not a complete government census or a measurement of lost production volume.

The report also puts logs at about 49% of combined hardwood log and lumber export volume in the first half of 2026, compared with roughly one-third in 2018. Absolute log volumes were broadly similar in the comparison; weaker lumber exports account for the larger log share.

Our business reading: refresh the actual buyer list for each species and grade. A closure count signals a changed network, but a company needs to know which outlets remain, their specifications, and what each delivery contributes after hauling. Avoid assuming that a national capacity contraction creates the same pricing opportunity in every local market.

Watch: active local outlets, product specifications and net delivered value.

U.S. / CANADA — SOFTWOOD LUMBER / FREIGHT

The latest lumber index slips; delivered cost remains the sales test

Madison’s October 9 update puts its Lumber Prices Index at US$500 per thousand board feet for the week ending October 9, down $5 from the prior week and $14 from one month earlier. This is an index, not a quote for every species, grade or shipment.

In its separate October 7 market commentary covering late September, Madison’s describes buyers accepting mill prices and then backing away when freight surcharges were included. It reports thin inventories, orders for specific immediate needs, and difficulty securing carriers. Keep that commentary’s period separate from the newer index observation.

Our operating interpretation: quote the full delivered order, with freight assumptions and a realistic delivery window, before treating mill-level interest as a completed sale. For a hauler, identify who bears changes in fuel cost and waiting time. For a mill or yard, confirm the lane and carrier availability alongside the tally. A lower headline lumber price does not by itself make the delivered purchase affordable.

Watch: total delivered price, carrier availability and the surcharge terms.

SOUTH — NORTH CAROLINA / OCTOBER 12

Timber-loss relief applications close Monday; assessments have a separate deadline

North Carolina’s second Timber Loss Relief Program application period closes Monday, October 12, at 5 p.m. local time. The program partially compensates eligible woodland owners for Hurricane Helene timber losses in 39 federally declared disaster counties. Qualifying owners must have at least 10 contiguous acres of impacted timber with moderate or greater storm damage.

Begin with the county Forest Service ranger, or the district office if county staff are unavailable. Owners who applied in the initial period do not need to reapply. Damage assessment reports for applications submitted during this second period are due October 26 at 5 p.m.; that date does not extend the application cutoff or reopen the initial period’s assessment deadline.

The agency says qualifying foresters can request technical-assistance funds before doing assessment work. Our practical priority for a new applicant is to establish the application’s status with the program office, then coordinate the required assessment and its submission. Use the linked forms and current program instructions.

CANADA — ONTARIO / OCTOBER 15

Forest Biomass intake: match the project to the stream and the cash flow

Ontario’s Forest Biomass Program lists its next application deadline as October 15, 2026, at 11:59 p.m.; the official page labels the time EST. Submit early and confirm the cutoff with the program office. Its streams cover innovative products, existing products, increased mobilization and modernization. Applicants must use the applicable form and supporting documents.

The program’s conditional contributions generally reimburse paid eligible expenses at agreed milestones. Advances are limited and discretionary. That makes the timing of spending and repayment part of the project’s feasibility, alongside stream-specific eligibility and funding limits.

Our business interpretation: a promising new outlet for forest biomass still needs a workable collection, processing and delivery plan. Before completing the application, connect the proposed investment to an identified bottleneck, document the project costs, and explain how the business will carry its share and the reimbursement interval. Confirm approved costs and agreement terms rather than assuming an application guarantees funding.

WEST — MONTANA / OCTOBER 21 BID

Tally Road Salvage calls for an on-site volume check

Montana DNRC’s GNA Tally Road Salvage sale offers approximately 465 tons of mixed-conifer sawlogs on the Flathead National Forest. Sealed bids open October 21 at 2 p.m. local time at 2705 Spurgin Road, Missoula. The package lists a minimum bid of $9.37 per ton and a $218 bid deposit; review the official forms, bond and payment obligations before submitting.

The prospectus strongly encourages bidders to visit the salvage units. Scattered blowdown pockets were not cruised to determine the volume estimate, and the advertised quantities are rough estimates. Operating periods depend on soil conditions. The harvest deadline is November 1, 2027; contract termination is December 31, 2027.

Our bidding interpretation: inspect recovery, access and handling time rather than treating the advertised tonnage as guaranteed saleable output. A relatively small salvage job can carry meaningful mobilization costs. Build the bid around the buyer’s own field observations, required work and expected delivered products.

Under the Radar

NORTHEAST — MAINE / HARVEST PRACTICES

New water-quality monitoring puts crossing execution in focus

The Maine Forest Service’s October 1 release summarizes 2024–2025 monitoring at 165 timber-harvesting sites, with more than 700 observations of individual activities. It reports appropriate stream-crossing practices at 74% of study sites, including planning that avoided crossings. In soil-movement observations, 85% showed no sediment entering a waterbody; the remaining 15% included measurable and trace amounts.

The agency links observed sedimentation most often to inadequate application of recommended Best Management Practices. The figures describe a monitored sample and defined observations, not a guarantee that every operation or crossing protected water quality.

Our field-management interpretation: make crossing selection, installation and follow-up inspection explicit parts of the job plan. A crew should know which areas to avoid, what drainage work is required, and who checks the installation after changing conditions. Training and a written specification become valuable when they produce consistent work on the ground.

MIDWEST — WISCONSIN / OCTOBER 18 WEEK

Forest Products Week offers a route into the supply chain

Wisconsin’s Forest Products Week begins Sunday, October 18. The DNR’s September announcement describes tours and special events at logging sites, sawmills, paper manufacturers, and secondary and urban-wood businesses. It directs readers to the Wisconsin Forestry Center for event information. Check the current event listing for the location, registration and access details of a particular visit.

Our business use: choose a visit that helps explain a part of the supply chain your customers or employees rarely see. A mill tour can make product specifications, recovery and residual uses more concrete; a logging visit can show how access and operating conditions affect delivery. Bring a focused question, and follow the host’s safety and participation requirements. These are learning opportunities, not an announcement that every facility is open for unregistered drop-ins.

FOREST BUSINESS SCHOOL RESOURCE

Make responder directions part of the job setup

The free Woods-Ready Safety Plan app helps loggers and foresters prepare a site-specific emergency response plan. It was built for the Northeastern Loggers’ Association with grant funding from the National Timber Harvesting and Transportation Safety Foundation.

For the next job, prepare the plan before the crew starts and review the location, access directions and emergency contacts together. Check that a new employee or visiting truck driver can describe how to reach the site. Revisit the plan when access changes or work moves to another location. The useful result is a crew that can find and use the information promptly.
