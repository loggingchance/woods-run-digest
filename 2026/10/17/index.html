U.S. / CANADA — SOFTWOOD LUMBER TRADE

Canadian lumber subsidy rates head for Monday publication

A U.S. Commerce Department notice on public inspection sets final Canadian softwood lumber countervailing-duty review rates. Dated October 13, it is scheduled for Federal Register publication on October 19. The notice ties new cash-deposit requirements to publication, rather than today’s date.

For the 2024 review, the notice lists 12.42% for Resolute and its named affiliates, 15.31% for West Fraser and its named affiliates, and 14.11% for 215 non-selected companies listed in an appendix. It separately retains 14.19% for other producers and exporters under the original all-others provision. These are countervailing-duty rates, not a combined total including antidumping duties or other tariffs. The document also distinguishes future deposit instructions from assessment of reviewed entries.

Our procurement interpretation: keep the producer, exporter, product and entry date attached to a quotation. A general industry percentage cannot establish the treatment of a particular shipment. Buyers and suppliers should review the actual published notice and applicable customs instructions with their trade specialists before revising landed-cost assumptions. Monday’s scheduled publication is the next checkpoint.

Watch: scheduled publication October 19; company-specific treatment and entry dates.

CANADA — SASKATCHEWAN / MILL CURTAILMENT

Big River’s curtailment reaches the layoff stage

Original reporting by paNOW on October 16 describes layoffs beginning at Carrier Forest Products’ Big River sawmill. At Friday’s press conference, opposition MLA Jordan McPhail said the first round began this week, with more expected in November and January.

Union representative Brandon Nicholson told the outlet that workers are seeking or taking other employment. In a written response, Saskatchewan’s government said rapid-response teams had visited Carrier five times and arranged follow-up appointments and training referrals. The company’s July announcement anticipated approximately 117 affected employees, an October 16 production curtailment and limited activity through January 2027. It described an indefinite curtailment without determining that the facility would close permanently.

Our supplier interpretation: confirm which deliveries, inventory movements and services remain authorized during the transition. Retained site activity does not establish normal log intake. A contractor needs a named contact, a current instruction and an agreed payment arrangement before assigning trucks or equipment. The new development is the reported start of layoffs; the broader curtailment plan was announced in July.

SOUTH — TENNESSEE / FORESTRY SKILLS

Youth forestry contest puts practical field skills in view

The University of Tennessee’s October 16 report names Monroe County’s Thomas Stroud, Levi West, William Warwick and Lucas Partin as the winning team at the state 4-H forestry competition. The contest took place October 10 at the UT Arboretum; the team will represent Tennessee at the national invitational.

Henderson County’s Ella Stanford earned the top individual score. Contest categories included tree, wood, insect and disease identification, compass and pacing, tree measurements, and a forest evaluation using a landowner scenario. UT credits families, coaches, Extension staff and volunteers for supporting the participants. Interested families can contact county Extension offices; the university says forestry participation begins at fourth grade.

Our workforce interpretation: these activities introduce observation, measurement and explaining a landowner’s choices—skills that can support later forestry training. A local business interested in helping can ask Extension what the existing program needs and how its staff can contribute appropriate experience. Competition results recognize learning already completed; they do not establish professional certification or an immediate pool of trained equipment operators.

MIDWEST — WISCONSIN / TODAY 9 A.M.–NOON

Antigo opens its sawmill and saw-filing shop to visitors

Northcentral Technical College hosts Breakfast at the Sawmill today, October 17, from 9 a.m. to noon at its Antigo campus, 312 Forrest Avenue. The official listing includes hands-on tours and activities at the Wood Technology Center of Excellence, featuring its new sawmill and saw-filing shop.

Visitors can also explore welding and healthcare labs and the Community Technology Center, and learn about programs, scholarships and financial aid. The Great Lakes Timber Professionals Association sponsors a free pancake breakfast while supplies last. The college describes a community event kicking off National Forest Products Week; it does not advertise a hiring fair or guarantee employment.

For local employers and prospective students, a facility visit can make a training conversation concrete. Ask which skills the equipment supports, how instruction fits the working week and whom to contact about the relevant program. A useful follow-up is a specific discussion between the learner, employer and college about the skills needed for a real role. Check the event listing before travelling.

Watch: today, 9 a.m.–noon in Antigo; breakfast while supplies last.

WEST — OREGON / RENEWAL OPENS MONDAY

Log-brand owners have a Monday address check

Oregon’s October 15 forestry notice says the five-year log-brand renewal period opens October 19 and runs through December 31, 2026. Renewal forms will be mailed Monday. Owners whose mailing addresses have changed should update the Log Brands Unit before that mailing.

Even brands first registered during 2026 must renew for the January 1, 2027–December 31, 2031 term. ODF’s standing program page lists a US$20 renewal fee. The agency’s notice describes registered identification brands for forest products and booming equipment transported on public highways, waterways or railroads west of the Cascade crest. Its program page provides forms and directory information, with the unit holding the most current registration status.

Our administrative use: check the registered owner and mailing address against the business’s records, then assign responsibility for the renewal and retain the confirmation. A change of business name or ownership may require the relevant separate form. Reach the unit at 503-931-5515 or log.brands@odf.oregon.gov for the applicable transaction. The immediate task is getting the notice to the right address.

Watch: October 19 mailing; December 31 renewal deadline.

NORTHEAST — MAINE / LANDOWNER TRAINING

Bucksport course gives woodland owners a five-evening starting point

Maine Forest Service’s current calendar lists a free Landowner 101 course in Bucksport for November 16–20, 2026, from 5:30 to 7:30 p.m. The five evening sessions meet at Bucksport High School, 102 Broadway. Registration is required through the official event page.

District foresters Steve McDonald and Allyssa Gregory will lead sessions with natural-resource professionals and organizations. The course addresses sustainable woodland management across objectives including forest products, firewood, wildlife, recreation, forest health and aesthetics. A field day will follow at a later date. Partners include RSU 25 Adult and Community Education, Maine Woodland Owners and Maine Tree Farm. The listing provides Gregory’s contact at 207-441-2895.

Our landowner use: arrive with the property’s basic information and a short account of what you want it to provide. Distinguishing family objectives, boundaries and stand conditions helps make later professional advice more useful. The advertised course is an educational starting point rather than a timber appraisal or a site-specific management prescription. It is a current registration opportunity; the calendar does not state a new announcement date.

UNDER THE RADAR — WOOD CHEMICALS / GERMANY

Leuna’s opening puts a wood-chemicals supply chain on display

UPM inaugurated its Leuna biorefinery in Germany on October 15. The company reports annual design capacity of 220,000 tonnes and total investment of €1.37 billion. Its release says enzymatic hydrolysis and lignin-derived specialty chemical production have reached stable operation, while product sales are ramping up.

The facility uses beechwood from thinnings and sawmill residues sourced within about 200 kilometres, described by UPM as unsuitable for higher-value timber or furniture uses. Processing produces industrial sugars and lignin for chemicals used in products including clothing, packaging, construction materials and tires. The inauguration is a milestone during ramp-up; the announcement does not establish that every process is operating at full design output.

Our fibre-market interpretation: a new material outlet becomes relevant through its feedstock specification, purchasing radius and dependable intake. Species and residue descriptions matter as much as the headline investment. For a North American operator assessing similar proposals, the questions are which materials qualify, how they are prepared and transported, and what sustained deliveries the buyer can accept. This German project is evidence of a particular industrial pathway, not a new local purchase offer.

UNDER THE RADAR — PELLET OPERATIONS / VIETNAM

A pellet case study connects output with the customer’s specification

An ANDRITZ customer case study describes Tây Sơn Biomass’s greenfield pellet operation in Vietnam. The producer reports 11–12 tonnes per hour from each BioMax pellet mill and currently has two machines, with a third being considered. These are customer-reported results, not independent performance guarantees.

The account connects equipment selection with a long-term Japanese power-generation offtake contract and European quality requirements. It describes wet and fine grinding with drying between stages, control of changes in raw material and infeed composition, and dies tailored to local material. The vendor’s current success-story index dates the feature October 14; the case study does not provide a precise plant commissioning date.

Our operating interpretation: compare output only alongside the material, energy input, product quality and accepted shipments. An equipment rate has commercial value when the rest of the process and the customer can support it. Before applying another mill’s result, establish a baseline using your own feedstock and specifications, identify the limiting step and agree how an equipment trial will measure acceptable production. The case supplies questions to test rather than a transferable production promise.
