CANADA — ONTARIO / NEW USES FOR WOOD FIBRE

Transformer insulation gives Canadian pulp a new industrial outlet

Hitachi Energy announced October 16 that it plans a C$479 million transformer-insulation factory in London, Ontario. The company expects the 21,000-square-metre facility to be completed by 2028 and create more than 100 permanent skilled jobs. Production will use wood fibre to make electrical insulation pressboard and laminates.

Canada’s federal announcement puts the project at C$478.8 million, including C$30 million in federal support: C$20 million through the Strategic Response Fund and C$10 million through Investments in Forest Industry Transformation. It lists capacity of up to 20,000 tonnes annually and says key inputs will be sourced domestically. Invest Ontario separately describes a conditional grant of up to C$20 million, subject to a definitive funding agreement.

Our fibre-market take: transformer demand can create a specialized outlet for pulp when the material consistently meets a demanding industrial specification. Prospective suppliers need to understand pulp quality, qualification, delivery and purchasing arrangements as the project develops. Construction and commissioning remain ahead; the useful near-term work is identifying the supply chain and its requirements. The company’s release explains the material’s role in electrical insulation and transformer cooling.

Watch: supplier qualification and project development toward expected completion in 2028.

Regional Timber Markets

MIDWEST — MINNESOTA / OCTOBER 29 AUCTION

Cass County’s auction asks bidders to plan access and cash together

Cass County’s current timber packet advertises an oral auction October 29 at 10 a.m. in the Upper Meeting Room in Backus, Minnesota. Seventeen tracts are offered: eleven intermediate and six regular. The county recommends registration before the auction through its Land Department.

Intermediate tracts are restricted to bidders with 30 or fewer full-time employees, and the packet limits each buyer to two intermediate tracts. Normally, the down payment is 20% of appraised value, due by the end of auction day. A bidder with a sale forfeiture in the past two years faces different terms. Individual tract sheets carry operating details and expiration dates; the first two specify winter access only.

Our bidding take: put the tract’s operating window beside its payment schedule before deciding what to pursue. Check whether the business can reach the timber during the permitted season, keep the crew supplied and fund the commitment. Appraisal and bid-up value serve different purposes in the terms. Read the full packet and confirm the meeting location and eligibility with the county at 218-947-3338.

NORTHEAST — MAINE / OCTOBER 21 WORKSHOP

Chester workshop addresses the weak points exposed by heavy rain

Maine Forest Service’s October 16 bulletin advertises a Forestry Best Management Practices for Extreme Weather workshop on October 21, from 9 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. The location is 16 Access Road in Chester, with W.T. Gardner & Sons hosting for the agency.

Designed for loggers and foresters, the workshop combines indoor presentations with an outdoor session. Its stated focus is controlling runoff, minimizing and stabilizing exposed mineral soil, and protecting infrastructure at forestry operations. Use the bulletin’s registration information to check participation arrangements and any remaining capacity before travelling.

Our operating take: bring a problem from an actual road, landing or crossing to the discussion. Where does runoff collect, where can exposed soil move, and which part of the infrastructure carries the greatest repair or interruption risk? A crew benefits when those observations translate into an assigned maintenance task and a follow-up inspection. The event offers a setting to compare practices with instructors and other operators. Site conditions and the applicable requirements still determine the work needed on a particular harvest.

Watch: October 21, 9 a.m.–2:30 p.m.; Chester workshop registration arrangements.

SOUTH — TENNESSEE / OCTOBER 20 BIDS

Chuck Swan hardwood offerings approach Tuesday’s opening

Tennessee’s current state-forest sales page lists two Chuck Swan hardwood offerings opening October 20, 2026. Sale A-04-27-01 covers 28 acres with an estimated 178,300 board feet; A-04-27-02 covers 58 acres with 389,400 board feet. The listed volumes use the Doyle rule.

The same page lists three Natchez Trace offerings for October 20 and provides invitations, pro forma contracts and a Place Bid contact process. Its Chuck Swan time-zone labels differ between the summary and table, so obtain the applicable invitation and confirm the opening time with the state. Follow the instructions for the individual sale when preparing and submitting a bid.

Our procurement take: use the days before opening to test the expected product mix against current mill specifications and hauling costs. A board-foot estimate is one input to a bid; access, sale conditions, crew timing and accepted destinations determine what the timber can contribute to the business. Keep the inspection notes and assumptions with the bid calculation so a change in price or operating conditions can be traced to its effect.

Watch: October 20; confirm the exact opening time and submission instructions.

MIDWEST — WISCONSIN / NOVEMBER 4 SEALED BIDS

NHAL offers twenty-three tracts with two delivery cutoffs

Wisconsin DNR’s current Northern Highland-American Legion package offers 23 timber tracts totaling 2,538 acres across Iron, Vilas and Oneida counties. The bid opening is November 4. The current public notice distinguishes delivery to the forestry office from later hand delivery at the opening location.

Bids reach Trout Lake Forestry Headquarters, 4125 Forest Headquarters Road, Boulder Junction, until 12:30 p.m. After that, bids may be handed to a DNR employee at the Boulder Junction Community Center until 1:30 p.m. The notice requires a separate bid and envelope for each tract. No bid bond is required, while a successful bidder must provide an acceptable performance bond within six weeks.

Our preparation take: rank the tracts only after checking the maps, product requirements and operating restrictions against the crew and receiving markets. Then assign someone to the submission itself. A sound stumpage estimate has little value if the paperwork arrives at the wrong place or time. Use the current notice for delivery instructions and call 715-904-1169 for the prospectus or clarification. Office delivery avoids leaving the move between locations to the last hour.

WEST — OREGON / OCTOBER 23 PLANNING

Oregon’s advisory agenda connects past harvests with future operations

Oregon announced October 16 that its State Forests Advisory Committee will meet October 23, beginning at 9 a.m. at ODF headquarters in Salem. The meeting is in Building C at 2600 State Street, with online participation available through the published agenda.

The agenda includes forest-management and habitat-conservation plan updates, fiscal-year 2026 accomplishments and fiscal-year 2028 annual operations planning. The accomplishments discussion covers harvests, stream restoration and young-stand management. Public comment is scheduled near the start of the meeting. Check the committee page and agenda for participation information and any changes.

Our planning take: contractors and wood buyers can use these discussions to understand implementation priorities and the sequence of future work. Track what has been completed, what still needs planning and which decisions lead to an advertised opportunity. That distinction helps a business decide when to inspect, ask a technical question or begin estimating. The committee discussion provides context for future operations; purchasing and contracting commitments come through their respective formal processes.

UNDER THE RADAR — CHIP LOGISTICS / GERMANY

Rail measurement moves from a yard process into supplier billing

Mercer’s October 15 report says Germany’s PTB has certified Dralle’s tScale optical rail portal for wood-chip deliveries. Mercer has used its automatically measured rail-delivery volumes for billing since September 1, 2026. The company says truck measurement had already received certification.

Mercer reports that truck and rail systems together now automatically record approximately half its incoming raw-material volume under the applicable metrology requirements. The system uses 3D cameras and artificial intelligence for material recognition. The account credits coordination among logistics, purchasing, the yard and IT with integrating the process into operating facilities.

Our transaction take: measurement changes matter to the supplier as well as the receiving yard. Before adopting a similar process, establish the measured quantity, conversion assumptions, calibration arrangements and how both parties can investigate an exception. Compare records from known loads and follow the data through to an invoice. The reported approval concerns this system’s use under German requirements. A North American installation would need its own applicable arrangements and evidence of performance with the materials it receives.

UNDER THE RADAR — U.S. FOREST CARBON / WORKING BETA

FACT puts forest and wood-product carbon information within reach

The Forestry Analytics for Carbon Tracking platform is available as a free working beta. The U.S. Endowment and Forest Service announced it October 5; its current project pages continue to invite testing and feedback. A full Version 1 release is planned for summer 2027.

FACT brings standardized U.S. forest and harvested-wood-product carbon information into a common platform. Its current navigation includes forest-stand carbon, landscape carbon, embodied carbon and a carbon-displacement factor. The harvested-wood-product carbon tool is marked as coming soon. Methodology pages explain the analytical approach, and the beta period allows functions and explanations to evolve.

Our analytical take: start with the question a customer or landowner actually needs answered, then check whether the tool’s geography, data and assumptions fit it. Retain the input values, method and date with the result so another person can follow the calculation. A useful screening estimate can help identify where better inventory or process data are needed. This is a resource review of an October launch; a particular reporting requirement still needs its own supporting evidence.
