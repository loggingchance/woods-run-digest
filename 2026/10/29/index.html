SOUTH — ALABAMA / ARKANSAS / OCTOBER 28 ANNOUNCEMENT

West Fraser plans two mill curtailments as housing demand stays weak

West Fraser will indefinitely curtail its Huguley oriented strand board mill in Lanett, Alabama, and its lumber mill in Leola, Arkansas. The October 28 announcement expects fourth-quarter wind-downs.

The actions would remove annual capacity of 500 million square feet of oriented strand board, measured on a three-eighths-inch basis, and 225 million board feet of southern yellow pine lumber. These are capacity reductions, not a statement that those volumes were being shipped.

The company cites continued weakness in new-home construction and repair-and-renovation demand. It reported a US$80 million third-quarter net loss on US$1.363 billion of sales and expects a US$200–220 million fourth-quarter asset impairment related to the curtailments.

Management says it will support employees through the transition. The release gives no affected-worker count or exact final operating date for either mill.

CANADA — FOREST MANAGEMENT / OCTOBER 28 COMPANY REPORT

Domtar describes changes from climate-risk assessment to field practice

Domtar’s October 28 account describes designated skid trails, drone seeding and changes to regeneration in its Canadian forests. The company says a 2022 climate-risk assessment helped set priorities.

In Ontario, it reports using drone seeding to limit the operating footprint and the need for additional herbicide treatment. Around its Windsor mill in Quebec, a hybrid-poplar program uses pulp-and-paper residuals: biosolids supply nutrients while lime-based material adjusts soil acidity.

After tornado damage near Lawrence Lake, Ontario, the company participated in a prescribed burn across more than 600 hectares. It reports reductions of 70–100% in fine fuels and 40–70% in heavier fuels, with improved regeneration conditions.

These are company-reported operational examples. The article does not provide a controlled comparison establishing the same fuel reductions, regeneration results or cost savings on other sites.

SOUTH — SOUTH CAROLINA / BIDS DUE NOVEMBER 18

Middle Enoree timber sale offers thinning and regeneration work

South Carolina’s Department of Natural Resources is seeking per-ton bids for its Middle Enoree 2026 timber sale in Sumter National Forest. Bids open November 18 at 11 a.m.; they must arrive before opening.

The current listing estimates 10,268 tons of pine sawtimber, 5,625 tons of pine chip-n-saw, 6,753 tons of pine pulpwood and 760 tons of hardwood pulpwood. The harvest includes 274.7 acres of thinning, 60.3 acres of clearcutting and 27.7 acres of shelterwood cutting.

Bidders submit a separate per-ton price for each product. The notice allows mailed or emailed bids, identifies the West Columbia opening location, and specifies a nine-month removal period and US$10,000 performance bond. Receipt, rather than a postmark, controls the deadline; the full notice contains the bidding and harvest conditions.

Under the Radar

NORTHEAST — MAINE / OCTOBER 28 REPORT

Maine retains ash-borer quarantine after opposition to repeal

Maine will keep its emerald ash borer quarantine after abandoning a proposed repeal, Maine Public reported October 28. State Horticulturist Gary Fish said more than 80 comments arrived, nearly all urging retention.

The Maine Forest Service’s current guidance still lists Chapter 275 and its regulated materials. They include hardwood firewood of every species, ash logs and specified other ash material, including green lumber with bark, live edge or wane.

The state says these materials cannot move from quarantined areas into areas outside the quarantine without a Maine Forest Service agreement. Its maps and material definitions remain the controlling references; the reporting does not establish a blanket certification requirement for every finished wood product.
