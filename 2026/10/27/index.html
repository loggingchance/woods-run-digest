SOUTH — NORTH CAROLINA / OCTOBER 26 REPORT

North Carolina sawtimber prices lag as pulpwood gains diverge

North Carolina’s third-quarter standing-timber prices remained below year-earlier levels for most sawtimber products, North Carolina State Extension reported October 26. Statewide pine chip-n-saw fell 16%, pine sawtimber 11% and pine ply logs 7%; oak and mixed hardwood sawtimber declined 4% and 9%, respectively.

Pulpwood moved differently: pine was up 10% and hardwood 25% from a year earlier. That does not mean prices rose throughout the latest quarter. Hardwood pulpwood fell 11% from the second quarter, while pine pulpwood gained 5%.

Regional differences were substantial. Eastern North Carolina hardwood pulpwood fell 21% quarter to quarter, while the western market rose 2%. The report cautions that aggregate prices do not value a particular stand: species, access, location and distance to mills matter.

CANADA — BRITISH COLUMBIA / OCTOBER 26 RESEARCH UPDATE

Forest residues get another route into higher-value materials

University of British Columbia wood scientist Feng Jiang has received an Arthur B. McDonald Fellowship supporting work on renewable materials made from underused forest residues. The university’s October 26 announcement describes lignocellulosic nanomaterials—tiny materials derived from plant fibres—produced using recyclable solvents.

The research addresses a recurring barrier: promising laboratory materials can be too expensive or resource-intensive to manufacture widely. Reported applications include insulating aerogels, biodegradable packaging, textiles and structural materials, with industry and First Nations partnerships helping translate the work.

This is research support, rather than an announced mill or purchase contract. The release provides no commercial production capacity, feedstock price or procurement timetable.

MIDWEST — MINNESOTA / INVENTORY UPDATED OCTOBER 26

Spring seedling orders remain open as some species sell out

Minnesota’s State Forest Nursery has updated its spring 2027 price list with several species sold out, including white cedar, bur oak and silver maple. Red pine, white pine and white spruce in the five-to-twelve-inch class remain listed without sold-out notices; actual inventory can change during ordering.

The regular minimum is 300 seedlings, mixing species in increments of 100. The listed conifers cost US$70 per hundred, with lower per-tree prices at larger quantities. A separate 250-seedling pine-and-spruce packet is listed at US$187.50.

The state’s announced sales window runs through March 12, 2027, or stock exhaustion. The live price list and nursery ordering link provide current options for reforestation and woodland planting; the original opening announcement supplies the window and planting-program details.

Under the Radar

NORTHEAST — NEW HAMPSHIRE / OCTOBER 25 REPORT

Bartlett’s forest research has support, but its future is unsettled

Bartlett Experimental Forest is not among the 23 sites identified for closure in the Forest Service’s latest review, but its staffing and facilities remain uncertain, the Concord Monitor reported in a story republished by New Hampshire Public Radio October 25.

After a recent stakeholder visit, Senator Jeanne Shaheen described discussions of university and other partnerships and a possible memorandum of understanding. The report also describes planned bunkhouse renovations supported through the Great American Outdoors Act.

Researchers stress that Bartlett’s long-running work informs forest maintenance and decisions by landowners, foresters and the timber industry. Stakeholders’ optimism does not amount to a final operating agreement; the report says no definite decision on Bartlett’s future has been reached.
