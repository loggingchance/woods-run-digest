MID-ATLANTIC — MARYLAND / HAULING COSTS

Maryland’s hauling relief retains a federal fuel limit

Maryland’s October 9 emergency order provides temporary state relief for qualifying agricultural and forestry haulers, including a waiver of fees for 30-day Exceptional Hauling Permits. Its dyed-diesel provision does not waive federal taxes, penalties or requirements.

The signed order defines forest products to include timber, logs, pulpwood and chips, while excluding processed lumber. Operators using its state fuel relief must be able to document qualifying hauling. The hauling-permit provisions retain vehicle and axle limits, bridge restrictions and safety requirements. Weight relief applies to qualifying loaded vehicles; it excludes interstate highways, local roads and Maryland Transportation Authority roads.

The initial emergency period is 30 days, with renewal possible. The administration says it is seeking federal penalty relief; that request is not the same as a federal waiver already being in force. Read the signed order linked in the governor’s release and confirm implementation with the responsible agencies before changing fuel or hauling practices.

Our business interpretation: identify the relief that actually applies to the company’s product, vehicle and route. A permit-fee waiver may reduce an expense without changing the trip’s other obligations. Keep route, load and fuel records together, and do not count a prospective federal action as a current operating saving.

Watch: eligibility, covered routes and any separately issued federal fuel relief.

CANADA — BRITISH COLUMBIA / VALUE-ADDED WOOD

Chemainus plans add manufacturing without announcing a sawmill restart

October 9 local reporting says Japan-based Shinohara Nexus Group expects a new Chemainus operation to create 30 jobs by early 2027, with plans to reach about 70 over three years. A public update from Mayor Rob Douglas, reproduced by councillor Chris Istace, describes a lease with Western Forest Products for two existing storage buildings on part of the sawmill site.

The mayor’s update says Western Forest Products advised the United Steelworkers that the new operation would not prevent the Chemainus sawmill from restarting. That statement preserves a possibility; it does not announce a restart or prove that the planned jobs are already filled.

Our market interpretation: new manufacturing can use an existing industrial location while serving a different part of the wood-products chain. Potential suppliers should establish the required input specifications, purchasing timetable and delivery arrangements. A component plant’s demand cannot be inferred directly from employment projections, and it need not equal the former sawmill’s log demand.

Watch: actual startup, purchasing specifications and distinct sawmill decisions.

CANADA — NEW BRUNSWICK / EXPORT LOGISTICS

Nackawic’s planned idling reaches beyond the mill gate

CTV’s October 6 report, excerpted by Tree Frog Forestry News, quotes Port Saint John CEO Craig Bell Estabrooks on the effects of AV Nackawic’s pending idling. He puts the mill at roughly 8%–10% of containerized traffic and as much as 15%–20% of export containers. These are different denominators: the export share is not the share of all cargo handled by the port.

The port is seeking other cargo business. The same account describes strength in break-bulk activity and steady cruise business. A pulp-related loss therefore should not be treated as an identical contraction across every port activity.

Our logistics interpretation: a major customer’s change can affect the surrounding trucking and export network as well as wood procurement. Confirm the actual lanes, shipment schedule and handling arrangements on which the business depends. Diversification may help a port overall while leaving a particular carrier or supplier with a much narrower set of replacement customers.

Watch: affected shipment lanes and replacement business at the company level.

MIDWEST — MICHIGAN / NOVEMBER 2 LOI

Rural Development Fund now has a two-stage 2027 intake

Michigan’s current Rural Development Fund page lists letters of intent due November 2, 2026, at 5 p.m. The full application opens December 2 and is due January 13, 2027, at 5 p.m. Confirm the time zone and submission details with the program office. This is an LOI decision now, not a full application due next month.

The 2027 FAQ explicitly includes forestry among land-based industries and allows for-profit businesses among eligible applicants, provided the project meets the requirements and serves an eligible rural area. Community and Business Development requests can reach $100,000; Regional Collaboration requests can reach $200,000. Applicants may submit only one LOI and one application per round across those categories.

The minimum match is 20% of the requested grant, payable through cash, in-kind contributions or both. Our planning interpretation: choose the category around the actual project and document its rural benefit, costs and match. A regional label alone does not establish that an individual business project belongs in the larger category.

NORTHEAST — NEW HAMPSHIRE / OCTOBER 22

A regional market roundtable offers a check on national headlines

UNH Extension’s Forest Products Markets Update is scheduled online for October 22, 2026, from 5 to 6:30 p.m. New Hampshire time. Registration is required for the Zoom session, and the event lists 1.5 SAF continuing-forestry-education credits. Experts representing a range of the state’s forest-products markets will discuss their observations and expectations.

The program is co-sponsored by the NH Division of Forests and Lands, NH Timberland Owners Association and NH Sustainable Forestry Initiative State Implementation Committee. Use the event page’s registration link rather than assuming that an earlier meeting link applies.

Our business use: bring a specific question about a product, buyer or delivery area. National prices and mill announcements establish context, but local acceptance, specifications and hauling distance determine a particular load’s value. Separate what speakers have observed from their expectations, then compare both with the company’s own recent quotes and deliveries.

SOUTH — NORTH CAROLINA / DEADLINE TOMORROW

Final application day approaches for Helene timber-loss relief

The NC Forest Service still lists October 12, 2026, at 5 p.m. local time as the close of its second Timber Loss Relief Program application period. This Sunday reminder retains the published deadline; do not assume it moves to another day. The program covers qualifying Helene timber losses in 39 disaster counties, with at least 10 contiguous impacted acres and moderate or greater damage.

Applicants begin with the county ranger or the supervising district office. Existing applicants from the first period do not reapply. For second-period applications, damage assessment reports have a separate October 26, 5 p.m. deadline.

Our immediate priority: confirm submission instructions and the status of the application with the program office, and arrange the assessment required for this round. The later report deadline is not additional time to start the application. Use the official forms and contacts rather than interpreting a disaster-relief headline as proof of eligibility or a guaranteed payment.

Under the Radar

WEST — WASHINGTON / WORKING-FOREST POLICY

King County seeks working-forest experience for its advisory commission

King County’s Rural Forest Commission recruitment closes October 31, 2026. The county’s September 30 announcement seeks members for the 2027–2030 term. Its representation includes rural forest landowners, local forest-product users, professional or academic foresters, tribes, rural cities, state DNR and people concerned with non-timber forest values.

This is a volunteer advisory role with a minimum three-year commitment. The commission helps shape county policy on working forests, wildfire hazard reduction and forest health. The county provides an application link and a contact for questions about the positions.

Our practical reading: people who work with forest access, small ownerships and product markets can bring evidence from daily operations into policy discussions. Before applying, review the role and time commitment, and consider a concrete example of how policy affects continued forest management. An advisory appointment is a contribution to local decision-making, not a timber contract or grant award.

WEST — WASHINGTON / OREGON / CALIFORNIA / RESEARCH

Burned area and lost forest biomass are different measures

An October 4 arXiv manuscript by Elliot S. Shannon and colleagues combines Forest Inventory and Analysis measurements with annual 30-metre canopy-cover data to estimate changes within fire perimeters on national-forest lands in Washington, Oregon and California. The abstract describes 6,239 fires from 2000–2022 and estimates 95.3 million metric tonnes of wildfire-associated live aboveground biomass loss and 2.39 million hectares of forest mortality area.

The authors emphasize substantial differences within fire perimeters. This item summarizes their research abstract; it does not claim an independent validation or a peer-review outcome. The estimates are model-based, cover specified lands and years, and do not measure merchantable timber available for salvage.

Our management interpretation: identify what a mapped figure actually measures before turning it into an operating assumption. Burn perimeter, forest mortality, biomass change and recoverable products answer different questions. A supply assessment still needs product condition, access, ownership requirements and costs at the job scale.

FOREST BUSINESS SCHOOL RESOURCE

Check the emergency plan when a job’s access changes

The free Woods-Ready Safety Plan app helps loggers and foresters prepare a site-specific emergency response plan. It was built for the Northeastern Loggers’ Association with grant funding from the National Timber Harvesting and Transportation Safety Foundation.

Use a change in access as a reason to review responder directions with the crew. A new landing, a closed gate or a different entry road can make yesterday’s directions less useful. Check the plan’s location, contacts and approach route before work resumes, and make sure crew members know how to find the information. Keep the emergency briefing connected to the actual site.
